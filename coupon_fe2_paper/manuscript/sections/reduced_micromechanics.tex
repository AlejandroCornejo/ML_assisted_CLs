\section{Projection-based reduced-order models}
\label{sec:reduced}
The learned energies of Section~\ref{sec:learned} remove the microscopic solve from online constitutive evaluation. To place their structural accuracy and cost against physics-based alternatives, Section~\ref{sec:coupon} also uses three projection-based microscopic surrogates as intrusive references. These models instead approximate the microscopic displacement field while retaining the underlying constituent law. Reduced coordinates decrease the number of equilibrium unknowns, and hyperreduction decreases the number of element evaluations. The three references are an affine HPROM, an iterative HPROM--ANN with adaptive integration, and its direct counterpart D-HPROM--ANN.

Figure~\ref{fig:reduced_model_hierarchy} summarizes the construction, from the offline RVE snapshots to the affine and nonlinear state representations and the three online routes, which share the same stress--tangent output.

\subsection{Periodic microscopic reference}
All three reduced evaluators derive from the periodic RVE problem of Section~\ref{sec:fom}, whose $N$-dimensional discretization defines the FOM. A PROM solves the same equilibrium problem in reduced coordinates, and an HPROM additionally hyperreduces its projected operators.

The reduced models use the independent state $\bm u\in\R^N$ obtained from the physical nodal displacement vector $\bm d\in\R^{N_{\rm dof}}$ after enforcing periodicity and removing rigid translation. In the deployed convention,
\begin{equation}
 \bm d(\bm u,\ee)=\bm T\bm u+\bm g_{\rm per}(\ee),
 \qquad \bm R(\bm u;\ee)=\bm T^T\bm f_{\rm int}
                 \bigl(\bm d(\bm u,\ee)\bigr)=\bm0.
 \label{eq:hdm_lifting}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm T$ identifies periodic degrees of freedom, and the lifting $\bm g_{\rm per}$ supplies the strain-dependent displacement jumps and anchor values of Section~\ref{sec:fom}, so any independent state $\bm u$ produces a physical field that satisfies the prescribed kinematic constraints. With $\bm K_{\rm phys}=\partial_{\bm d}\bm f_{\rm int}$ the assembled physical stiffness, the FOM tangent is $\bm K=\partial_{\bm u}\bm R=\bm T^T\bm K_{\rm phys}\bm T$, and all residuals follow the internal-force sign convention.

The reduced bases are built from snapshots of this affine-retaining state, which relates to the fluctuation coordinates of Eq.~\eqref{eq:microeq} through $\bm a=\bm u-\bm u_{\rm aff}(\ee)$, with $\bm u_{\rm aff}$ the affine displacement at the independent nodes. This choice leaves the periodic boundary conditions unchanged and keeps the load-correlated information used by the strain-informed identification below, so the reported POD dimension characterizes this representation and its truncation tolerance.

\subsection{POD approximation and the affine PROM}
The first reduction targets the number of equilibrium unknowns. FOM solutions $\bm u^s$ at sampled strains $\ee^s$, $s=1,\ldots,N_s$, are centered about a reference state $\bm u_{\rm ref}$, and the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the snapshot matrix
\begin{equation}
 \bm S_{\rm snap}=
 [\,\bm u^1-\bm u_{\rm ref},\ldots,\bm u^{N_s}-\bm u_{\rm ref}\,]
 =\bm U_S\bm\Sigma_S\bm Y_S^T.
 \label{eq:snapshot_svd}
\end{equation}
supplies the POD modes. The $n_{\rm tra}$ leading left singular vectors form the orthonormal reduced-order basis (ROB) $\bm V_{\rm tra}$ of the affine approximation
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\bm u}=\bm u_{\rm ref}+\bm V_{\rm tra}\qq_{\rm tra},
 \qquad \qq_{\rm tra}\in\R^{n_{\rm tra}},
 \qquad \bm V_{\rm tra}^T\bm V_{\rm tra}=\bm I.
 \label{eq:linear_prom}
\end{equation}
which replaces the $N$ independent displacement coordinates by $n_{\rm tra}\ll N$ modal amplitudes. The subscript ``tra'' marks this traditional affine approximation, and the term affine refers only to the approximation space, since the constitutive law and the reduced equilibrium equations remain nonlinear.

The retained dimension is selected from the relative squared reconstruction error, which for a Euclidean POD is
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_{\rm POD}^2(n_{\rm tra})=
 \frac{\sum_{j>n_{\rm tra}}\sigma_j^2}{\sum_j\sigma_j^2}.
 \label{eq:pod_tail}
\end{equation}
This criterion measures how well the snapshots are compressed but does not bound the stress error at unsampled strains, because force evaluation, constitutive differentiation, and equilibrium conditioning can make small displacement components important.

Galerkin projection onto the admissible variations $\delta\widetilde{\bm u}=\bm V_{\rm tra}\delta\qq_{\rm tra}$ gives the reduced equations and tangent at a prescribed strain $\ee$,
\begin{equation}
 \bm r_{\rm tra}(\qq_{\rm tra};\ee)
 =\bm V_{\rm tra}^T
  \bm R(\bm u_{\rm ref}+\bm V_{\rm tra}\qq_{\rm tra};\ee)=\bm0,
 \quad
 \bm K_{\rm tra}=\bm V_{\rm tra}^T\bm K\bm V_{\rm tra}.
 \label{eq:linear_galerkin}
\end{equation}
The system now has $n_{\rm tra}$ unknowns, but evaluating $\bm R$ and $\bm K$ still visits every microscopic element, which the hyperreduction of Section~\ref{sec:hyperreduction} addresses.

\subsection{Nonlinear reduction through latent-space closure}
\label{sec:latent_closure}
The POD dimension required to reconstruct a displacement field can exceed the number of coordinates needed to parametrize the loading family. Latent-space closure exploits this distinction by retaining a rich linear span while learning dependencies among its modal amplitudes, so that only a smaller subset remains unknown in equilibrium~\cite{barnett2023,aresdeparga2026nonlinear}. An $n_{\rm tot}=n+\bar n$ dimensional ROB is partitioned into orthonormal primary and secondary blocks,
\begin{equation}
 \bm V_{\rm tot}=[\,\bm V,\overline{\bm V}\,],\qquad
 \bm V^T\bm V=\bm I,\quad
 \overline{\bm V}^T\overline{\bm V}=\bm I,\quad
 \bm V^T\overline{\bm V}=\bm0,
 \label{eq:partitioned_rob}
\end{equation}
and each snapshot is projected onto the corresponding coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \qq^s=\bm V^T(\bm u^s-\bm u_{\rm ref}),
 \qquad
 \overline{\qq}^{\,s}=\overline{\bm V}^T(\bm u^s-\bm u_{\rm ref}).
\end{equation}
The primary coordinates $\qq\in\R^n$ remain independent unknowns, whereas an ANN closure $\mathcal N:\R^n\to\R^{\bar n}$ predicts the secondary coordinates. The resulting nonlinear decoder and its tangent are
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\bm u}(\qq)=\bm u_{\rm ref}+\bm V\qq
       +\overline{\bm V}\mathcal N(\qq;\bm\eta),
 \qquad
 \bm B(\qq)=\partial_{\qq}\widetilde{\bm u}
       =\bm V+\overline{\bm V}\mathcal N_{,\qq}.
 \label{eq:prom_ann}
\end{equation}
The ANN predicts modal amplitudes of the microscopic displacement, not stress or energy, and the secondary modes remain in that field without appearing as independent equilibrium unknowns.

The closure is trained on snapshot pairs $(\qq^s,\overline{\qq}^{\,s})$ by minimizing
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm cl}(\bm\eta)=
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal I_{\rm fit}|\bar n}
 \sum_{s\in\mathcal I_{\rm fit}}
 \|\mathcal N(\qq^s;\bm\eta)-\overline{\qq}^{\,s}\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:closure_loss}
\end{equation}
Input standardization is absorbed into the closure map, while the secondary outputs remain unscaled. Smooth $\tanh$ activations provide the decoder derivatives required by projection. This coordinate loss measures displacement reconstruction within the secondary space.

The leading POD coefficients need not provide a well-conditioned primary-to-secondary map. The input-informed construction of Hernández et al.~\cite{hernandez2026dimensional} instead combines the retained modal amplitudes linearly into three primary coordinates $\bm\xi$ approximately aligned with the three macroscopic strain components. If $\bm A_m$ denotes the resulting nonsingular coordinate transformation, the deployed decoder is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\bm u}(\bm\xi)=\bm u_{\rm ref}
   +\bm V\bm A_m\bm\xi
   +\overline{\bm V}\widehat{\mathcal N}(\bm\xi),
 \qquad
 \bm B_\xi=\partial_{\bm\xi}\widetilde{\bm u}.
 \label{eq:deployed_decoder}
\end{equation}
Because the alignment $\bm\xi^s\approx\ee^s$ is only fitted from the snapshots, the iterative PROM--ANN solves at fixed $\ee$ for the state satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \bm r(\bm\xi;\ee)=\bm B_\xi^T
       \bm R(\widetilde{\bm u}(\bm\xi);\ee)=\bm0,
 \label{eq:manifold_galerkin}
\end{equation}
rather than imposing $\bm\xi=\ee$. Because $\bm B_\xi$ depends on the state, the exact Newton derivative includes the curvature of the decoder manifold. Appendix~\ref{app:latent_closure} gives the coordinate construction, the reconstruction-error split, and this tangent explicitly.

For the comparison below, the affine and nonlinear models share the same retained span, $n_{\rm tot}=n_{\rm tra}$, and the nonlinear model reduces the number of equilibrium unknowns from $n_{\rm tra}$ to $n=3$ before any hyperreduction. For compactness, the cubature and output formulas below retain $\qq$ for the primary modal amplitudes, and the deployed nonlinear model evaluates the decoder and adaptive-weight maps through $\bm\xi=\bm A_m^{-1}\qq$.

\subsection{Hyperreduction of the projected equations}
\label{sec:hyperreduction}
Hyperreduction replaces each full element sum in the projected equations with a sparse weighted sum, a second approximation introduced after the displacement representation has been chosen. Two integration tasks must be distinguished: assembling the projected equilibrium equations and recovering the homogenized stress. Hernández et al.~\cite{hernandez2026dimensional} formulate both tasks and discuss the use of either a common or a dedicated output rule. Separate elementwise rules are used here because the two integrands and their accuracy requirements differ.

\subsubsection{Fixed elementwise cubature}
Let $\mathcal E$ be the microscopic element set and $\bm L_e$ extract element nodal values from $\bm d$. At fixed macroscopic input, define the reconstructed element state and its reduced tangent by
\begin{equation}
 \bm d_e=\bm L_e(\bm T\widetilde{\bm u}+\bm g_{\rm per}),
 \qquad \bm B_e=\bm L_e\bm T\bm B .
\end{equation}
The full reduced residual is $\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\bm h_e$, where
$\bm h_e=\bm B_e^T\bm f_{{\rm int},e}$. Fixed ECM approximates it as
\begin{equation}
 \bm r_{\rm ECM}(\qq;\ee)=
       \sum_{e\in\mathcal Z_r}w_e\bm h_e(\qq;\ee)=\bm0,
 \quad \mathcal Z_r\subset\mathcal E,\quad w_e\geq0.
 \label{eq:ecm}
\end{equation}
For the affine HPROM, which contains no ANN, $\bm B$ is replaced by $\bm V_{\rm tra}$ and the unknown is $\qq_{\rm tra}$. A selected element retains its ordinary within-element quadrature, so selected elements, selected Gauss points, and independent coordinates are distinct counts.

Offline, the element contributions are collected over $N_c$ training states and compressed by SVD. ECM then selects a sparse support and nonnegative multipliers that reproduce the retained integration modes~\cite{hernandez2017}. If $\bm G_{\rm cub}$ denotes those modes augmented by the constant-integration constraint (Appendix~\ref{app:cubature}) and $\bm b_{\rm cub}=\bm G_{\rm cub}\bm1$, selection stops when
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\|\bm G_{\rm cub}\bm w-\bm b_{\rm cub}\|_2}
      {\|\bm b_{\rm cub}\|_2}\leq\varepsilon_{\rm ECM}.
 \label{eq:ecm_tolerance}
\end{equation}
The tolerance $\varepsilon_{\rm SVD}$ controls the preceding integrand compression, whereas $\varepsilon_{\rm ECM}$ controls the sparse fit. For the residual rule, the element data are $\bm h_e$. For the output rule, they are the four components of the element contribution to the average first Piola--Kirchhoff stress, all four of which are retained because this tensor is not generally symmetric. Both rules impose $\sum_e w_e=N_{\rm el}$, with physical quadrature measures already included in the element contributions. Their supports and weights remain fixed online.

\subsubsection{Manifold-adaptive weights}
\label{sec:adaptive_weights}
A fixed rule must represent every sampled state with the same multipliers. MAW--ECM instead retains one common support but lets its weights vary over the reduced manifold~\cite{hernandez2026dimensional}. Applied to the residual, this gives
\begin{equation}
 \bm r_{\rm MAW}(\qq;\ee)=
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal Z_r}w_e(\qq)\bm B_e(\qq)^T
                   \bm f_{{\rm int},e}(\bm d_e)=\bm0.
 \label{eq:maw}
\end{equation}
Starting from a fixed ECM rule, the MAW--ECM pruning algorithm removes elements while redistributing nonnegative statewise weights, through the local and graph-regularized stages described by Hernández et al.~\cite{hernandez2026dimensional}. Pruning identifies the support but not the continuous online map, and neural fields are used here for that final regression stage. Because the equilibrium and stress rules are separate, two maps, $\mathcal N_r$ and $\mathcal N_s$, are trained on independently pruned supports $\mathcal Z_r$ and $\mathcal Z_s$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \bm w(\bm\xi)&=N_{\rm el}\operatorname{softmax}
                 \bigl(\mathcal N_r(\bm\xi;\bm\theta_r)\bigr),
   &\sum_{e\in\mathcal Z_r}w_e&=N_{\rm el},\\
  \bm v(\bm\xi)&=N_{\rm el}\operatorname{softmax}
                 \bigl(\mathcal N_s(\bm\xi;\bm\theta_s)\bigr),
   &\sum_{e\in\mathcal Z_s}v_e&=N_{\rm el}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:softmax_weights}
\end{equation}
Input standardization is absorbed into each network. Unlike the closure ANN $\mathcal N$, which predicts secondary displacement coordinates, these networks predict integration weights, fitted against full-mesh element sums at each decoded training state with the loss of Appendix~\ref{app:cubature}. The scaled softmax guarantees positivity and normalization, whereas integration accuracy is learned. Offline, the closure is trained first, the two supports are then selected and pruned, and the two weight fields are fitted last.

Online, the current reduced iterate is passed to both the closure and $\mathcal N_r$, and $\mathcal N_s$ supplies the output weights, with the supports and all network parameters fixed. Differentiating the adaptive residual produces three contributions: element stiffness, decoder curvature, and weight variation. Only the first enters the microscopic modified-Newton iteration, which changes the convergence rate but not the converged state, whereas the consistent constitutive tangent differentiates the complete implemented residual and output, as described next. For the affine HPROM, the decoder curvature and weight-variation terms vanish, so its projected stiffness is the exact residual Jacobian.

To translate sparse cubature into wall-clock savings, each optimized reduced mesh contains only the selected elements and the nodes they require. The corresponding basis operators and periodic lifting are restricted to that support before online evaluation. Online cost therefore depends on the support size, the retained secondary dimension, the equilibrium iterations, and the derivative evaluation, and not only on the number of primary coordinates.

\subsection{Constitutive output}
\label{sec:reduced_outputs}
\subsubsection{Equilibrium-based stress and consistent tangent}
The reduced microscopic solver must return the same work-conjugate stress and tangent required by the FE$^2$ problem. The final stage of Figure~\ref{fig:reduced_model_hierarchy} summarizes this common output path. The separate output rule introduced above reconstructs the average first Piola--Kirchhoff stress as
\begin{equation}
 \bm P_h(\qq,\ee)=
 \frac1{|Y|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal Z_s}v_e(\qq)
        \sum_{g\in e}\omega_g\bm P_{\mu,g}(\bm d_e),\qquad
 \Sg_h=\F^{-1}\bm P_h .
 \label{eq:hyperreduced_stress}
\end{equation}
Here $v_e$ is fixed for HPROM and state dependent for HPROM--ANN. Because the independently fitted output rule need not produce an exactly symmetric converted tensor, the implementation returns its symmetric work-conjugate components,
\begin{equation}
 \ssv_h(\qq,\ee)=
 \left((S_h)_{11},\,(S_h)_{22},\,
 \tfrac12[(S_h)_{12}+(S_h)_{21}]\right)^T,
 \label{eq:reduced_stress_vector}
\end{equation}
and differentiates this actual output. The residual and stress supports may overlap. Their union defines the element data required by the iterative constitutive evaluation, whereas only $\mathcal Z_r$ enters the equilibrium solve.

Reduced equilibrium defines an implicit state $\qq^*(\ee)$ and hence the constitutive map $\ssv_h(\qq^*(\ee),\ee)$. If the residual Jacobian is nonsingular, differentiating equilibrium gives
\begin{equation}
 \bm D_h=\ssv_{h,\ee}
       -\ssv_{h,\qq}\bm r_{h,\qq}^{-1}\bm r_{h,\ee}.
 \label{eq:reduced_ift}
\end{equation}
The partial derivatives hold the other argument fixed and are evaluated at the converged state. They include the periodic lifting, stress conversion, decoder curvature, and, for adaptive rules, weight variation. Equation~\eqref{eq:reduced_ift}, rather than the modified-Newton matrix used to reach microscopic equilibrium, supplies the tangent returned to the structural solver. Appendix~\ref{app:cubature} records how these derivatives are evaluated in the two implementations.

\subsubsection{Direct decoder evaluation: D-HPROM--ANN}
When input-informed coordinates closely approximate the loading parameters, the decoder can also be evaluated without solving reduced equilibrium, as discussed by Hernández et al.~\cite{hernandez2026dimensional} for their hyperelastic example. Here this defines a separate operating mode, D-HPROM--ANN, obtained by prescribing $\bm\xi=\ee$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:deployed_decoder},
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\bm u}_{D}(\ee)=\bm u_{\rm ref}
       +\bm V\bm A_m\ee
       +\overline{\bm V}\widehat{\mathcal N}(\ee).
 \label{eq:direct_decoder}
\end{equation}
The decoded state is inserted directly into the adaptive stress rule, using the same closure and stress-weight field as the iterative model. Only the stress support is needed, since no residual cubature or reduced equilibrium Jacobian is evaluated. The tangent is obtained by central differences of the complete direct stress map, updating the decoder, weights, lifting, and stress conversion at each perturbed input.

The accuracy of D-HPROM--ANN depends on both the closure and the approximation $\bm\xi\approx\ee$. Conversely, solving projected equilibrium corrects the primary state only within the chosen manifold and cubature approximation, so it need not give a smaller stress error than direct evaluation at every input.

\subsection{Mechanical structure retained by the reduced models}
Unlike a direct constitutive surrogate, every reduced model above continues to evaluate the original microscopic hyperelastic law, and the iterative variants also enforce projected equilibrium. This preserves a direct micromechanical link, but it does not automatically make the deployed macroscopic stress the derivative of an effective potential.

For fixed weights and consistent kinematics, Galerkin internal forces can be derived from a weighted reduced energy, as in the structure-preservation results established for ECSW under their stated hypotheses~\cite{farhat2015}. Two features of the present deployment prevent that conclusion from transferring without further assumptions. First, equilibrium and stress use independently fitted cubature rules, so they need not derive from the same energy. Second, when the weights depend on the reduced state, weighting energy derivatives is not equivalent to differentiating the weighted energy, because
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_{\qq}\sum_e w_e(\qq)\Pi_e(\qq,\ee)
 =
 \sum_e w_e(\qq)\nabla_{\qq}\Pi_e
 +\sum_e\Pi_e\nabla_{\qq}w_e .
 \label{eq:weight_energy_difference}
\end{equation}
The final term is absent from Eq.~\eqref{eq:maw}, and nonnegative weights do not eliminate it. Consequently, no exact effective potential or polyconvexity certificate is claimed for the deployed HPROM variants, whose consistent tangent is the derivative of the implemented stress map and need not be symmetric.
