        Computational homogenization analysis in finite plasticity
      Simulation of texture development in polycrystalline materials

Abstract

   The paper presents a framework for the treatment of a homogenized macro-continuum with locally attached micro-structure, which
undergoes non-isothermal inelastic deformations at large strains. The proposed concept is applied to the simulation of texture evolution in
polycrystalline metals, where the micro-structure consists of a representative assembly of single crystal grains. The deformation of this
micro-structure is coupled with the local deformation at a typical material point of the macro-continuum by three alternative constraints of
the microscopic fluctuation field. In a deformation driven process, extensive macroscopic variables, like stresses and dissipation are defined
as volume averages of their microscopic counterparts in an accompanying local equilibrium state of the micro-structure. The proposed
numerical implementation is based in the general setting on a finite element discretization of the macro-continuum which is locally coupled
at each Gauss point with a finite element discretization of the attached micro-:~tructure. In the first part of the paper we set up the two
coupled boundary value problems associated with the macro-continuum and the pointwise attached micro-structure and consider aspects of
their finite element solutions. The second part presents details of a robust algorithmic model of finite plasticity for single crystals which
governs the response of the grains in a typical micro-structure. The paper concludes with some representative numerical examples by
demonstrating the performance of the proposed concept with regard to the prediction of texture evolution in polycrystals. © 1999 Elsevier
Science S.A. All rights reserved.




Introduction

  The paper presents a theoretical and computational framework for the treatment of a homogenized macro-
continuum with locally attached micro-structure as visualized in Fig. 1, which undergoes non-isothermal
inelastic deformations at large strains. The proposed concept is applied to the simulation of texture
developments in polycrystalline metals, where the micro-structure, consists of a representative assembly of single
crystal grains.
   The macroscopic thermomechanical description of heterogeneous materials such as polycrystals can be based
on the introduction of at least two different phenomenological scales. In this context, the macro-scale is usually
associated with a homogenized continuous medium. The micro-scale is often characterized by a statistically
representative volume which contains micro-structural constituents. For an idealized polycrystal, these
constituents are defined by the single crystal grains. With regard to the prediction of texture developments in
polycrystalline materials, a direct constitutive modelling on the macro-scale turns out to be very complicated.
Effects of anisotropy due to the crystallographic texture evolution, i.e. the reorientation of the crystal lattices of
the grains during the deformation, are very hard to describe within the direct macroscopic approach. As a
consequence, many existing macroscopic material models are a priori restricted to isotropic response. Advanced
models usually take into account Bauschinger's effect of kinematic hardening but neglect the evolution of the

   In contrast, ~the constitutive modelling of the crystal grains on a typical representative micro-scale is much
better understood. Here, the so-called continuum slip theory has achieved a stage of common acceptance in
recent years, see for instance the work of Kr6ner [1], Teodosiu [2], Rice [3], Mandel [4], Teodosiu and Sidoroff
[5], Hill and Havner [6], Havner [7,8] and Asaro [9]. Many constitutive hardening laws for the evolution of the
single slip in crystals based on kinetic dislocation models are reported in the literature. We refer in this context
to Kocks [10], Kocks et al. [11], Hutchinson [12], Chang and Asaro [131, Hull and Bacon [14], Cuitifio and
Ortiz [ 15], Bassani [ 16] and the references therein. These laws serve as the cornerstone for the application of the
continuum slip theory to the description of the inelastic deformations of crystal grains. The simplicity and
transparency of the continuum slip theory has motivated many authors to describe the overall response of a
polycrystal by a micro-macro-transition from a representative micro-structure to the macro-continuum on the
basis of a homogenization technique. With regard to the simulation of texture evolutions, we refer to Asaro and
Needleman [171, Harren and Asaro [18], Mathur and Dawson [19], Kalidindi et al. [20], Kumar and Dawson
[21], Kothari and Anand [22]. Many of these works apply--with different individual accents--the so-called
Taylor-type micro-macro-transition, which can be traced back to the pioneering work of Taylor [23]. Here, the
basic idea is to assume a kinematic constraint of the deformation of the micro-structure such that it is at all
points identical to the local deformation of the macro-continuum. The macroscopic extensive variables are then
defined by volume averages of their microscopic counterparts. This approach yields in many situations an
overestimated :;tiff response of the micro-structure due to the rigorous kinematic constraint. Clearly,
inhomogeneous deformations of the micro-structure, effects like debounding of the grains or nucleation of voids
are excluded in this concept.
   The first goal of this paper is to place the Taylor-type approach in a more general context by considering
alternative micro-macro-transitions. To this end, homogenization approaches based on average theorems as
outlined for example by Hill [24,25], Hassanizadeh and Gray [26], Suquet [27], Krawietz [28], Maugin [29,
Nemat-Nasser and Hori [30] are recast into a straightforward computational procedure for a direct evaluation of
micro-macro-transitions in finite thermoplasticity. The basic concept is to endow the macro-continuum locally
with a polycrystalline micro-structure, i.e. a well-defined aggregate of single crystal grains as indicated in Fig. 1.
The deformation of this micro-structure is then coupled with tile local deformation at a typical material point of
the macro-continuum. In a process driven by the deformation of the macro-continuum, this coupling can be
defined by three alternative constraints of the micro-structure deformation: (i) zero fluctuations in the domain
(Taylor-type assumption); (ii) zero fluctuations on the boundary; or (iii) periodic fluctuations on the boundary.
These boundary conditions induce in combination with a given constitutive model of finite thermoplasticity for
single crystals a static equilibrium state of the micro-structure at a certain stage of the deformation process. The
macroscopic exte,nsive variables such as stresses and the dissipation are then defined as volume averages of their
microscopic counterparts defined by the equilibrium state of the micro-structure. In the computational procedure
proposed here, we evaluate these averages in a straightforward manner. This covers the set-up and the solution
of two locally coupled boundary value problems for the finite deformations of the micro-continuum and the
                         C. Miehe et al. / Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Engrg. 171 (1999) 3 8 7 - 4 1 8           389


pointwise associated micro-structure, respectively. The numerical implementation is based in the general setting
on a nonlinear finite element analysis of the macro-continuum which is locally coupled at each Gauss point with
a nonlinear finite element analysis of the attached micro-structure. Clearly, this approach represents a large-scale
 computation which depends critically on the mesh size of the micro-structure. We regard the proposed concept
 as a numerical laboratory for the investigation of macroscopic phenomena of materials with complex
 micro-structures. It can be applied not only to the analysis of polycrystals but also to general composites or
 granular materials.
   The foundation for an application of the computational homogenization procedure outlined above is a
well-defined constitutive model of single crystal thermoplasticity which governs the response of the grains of
the micro-structure, As a consequence, the second goal of the w.per is the development of a robust stress update
algorithm for crystal plasticity. It is well known that crystal plasticity can be recast into the mathematical
framework of multisurface plasticity as outlined for example in the works of Koiter [31] and Mandel [4]. In this
format, the multiple constraints are the yield criterion functions on the given crystallographic slip planes. In
contrast to standard formulations of multisurface plasticity, these constraints can be linearly dependent. This
results within the rate-independent setting in a possible non-uniqueness of the set of active systems for a given
deformation mode, see also Kocks [10] and Havner [8] for a ,discussion of this point. The recent research on
computational single-crystal plasticity focuses on formulations with elastic domain and in particular the
 rate-independent theory. We refer in this context to Cuitifio and Ortiz [15], Borja and Wren 132], Anand and
Kothari [33] and Miehe [34,35]. Following this line, we here propose a new unified fully implicit multisurface-
type return algorithm for both the rate-independent and the rate-dependent setting, endowed with three
alternative physically motivated approaches to the regularization of possible redundant slip activities. This
includes the application of general inverses of the Jacobian of the currently active slip systems, originally
proposed by Anand and Kothari [33] and modified by Schr6der and Miehe [36], as well as a new viscoplastic
perturbation technique, motivated by the limit of the rate-dependent theory. It turns out that the latter approach
is the most efficient algorithmic implementation of rate-independent single crystal plasticity. We represent the
constitutive model exclusively within a geometric setting relative to the plastic intermediate configuration of the
local deformation of the micro-structure. With regard to an application to f.c.c, crystals, we specify the model to
cubic elastic symmet O, within the context of a thermoplastic kinetic slip model with latent hardening effects. A
further important contribution with regard to an efficient solution of the nonlinear boundary-problem of the
micro-structure with Newton-type solvers is the closed-form representation of the algorithmic (consistent)
tangent moduli for the general anisotropic finite plasticity model under consideration.
   The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we set up the general computational procedure outlined above
for micro-macro-transitions by considering the two coupled boundary value problems for the deformation of the
macro-continuum and the associated micro-structure and their solution by means of finite element methods.
Sections 3 and 4 deal with details of the theoretical set-up and the algorithmic implementation of a finite crystal
plasticity model. The three above-mentioned regularization techniques for the rate-independent limit are
discussed in Section 5. Finally, Section 6 considers some representative numerical examples which demonstrate
the performance of the proposed concept. Here, we compare results of the Taylor-type approach with the general
micro-macro-transitions with regard to the simulation of texture developments and demonstrate the performance
of the proposed algorithmic model of single-crystal plasticity.

finite deformations of the micro-continuum and the associated micro-structure as indicated in Fig. 2. In the
subsequent development we first outline the definition of the two coupled boundary value problems in the
context of a thermomechanical operator split and consider aspects of the associated finite element discretization.
Finally, in the last subsection we summarize the homogenization algorithm for the evaluation of volume-
averages.

7. Conclusion

  We have proposed a theoretical and computational concept for the implementation of alternative micro-
macro-transitions in finite plasticity under non-isothermal conditions. The concept is based on a coupled
                               C. Miehe et al. / Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Engrg. 171 (1999) 387-418                                       417


discretization of both the macro-continuum and a pointwise attached micro-structure and exploits in a
straightforward manner volume averaging theorems. This includes the classical Taylor-type micro-macro-
transition as a special case. The computational approach has been applied to the simulation of the texture
evolution in polycrystals under isothermal and non-isothermal conditions. To this end, a unified and robust fully
implicit stress update algorithm for single crystal thermoplasticity has been developed in the context of a
thermo-kinetic slip model. The numerical examples indicate that the Taylor-type micro-macro-transition yields
good results for the prediction of texture developments in standard situations without significant inhomogeneous
deformation mechanisms of the polycrystalline micro-structure. Future work will focus on inhomogeneous
deformations of polycrystalline micro-structures by taking into account effects such as debounding of the crystal
grains.
