                                                   SUMMARY
A new approach for the dimensional reduction via projection of nonlinear computational models based on the
concept of local reduced-order bases is presented. It is particularly suited for problems characterized by dif-
ferent physical regimes, parameter variations, or moving features such as discontinuities and fronts. Instead
of approximating the solution of interest in a fixed lower-dimensional subspace of global basis vectors, the
proposed model order reduction method approximates this solution in a lower-dimensional subspace gener-
ated by most appropriate local basis vectors. To this effect, the solution space is partitioned into subregions,
and a local reduced-order basis is constructed and assigned to each subregion offline. During the incremental
solution online of the reduced problem, a local basis is chosen according to the subregion of the solution
space where the current high-dimensional solution lies. This is achievable in real time because the compu-
tational complexity of the selection algorithm scales with the dimension of the lower-dimensional solution
space. Because it is also applicable to the process of hyper reduction, the proposed method for nonlinear
model order reduction is computationally efficient. Its potential for achieving large speedups while main-
taining good accuracy is demonstrated for two nonlinear computational fluid and fluid-structure-electric
interaction problems. Copyright © 2012 John Wiley & Sons, Ltd.

                                             1. INTRODUCTION

Many model order reduction (MOR) techniques are based on the following simple but powerful
observation: very often, the trajectory of a large-scale discrete system belongs to an affine subspace
whose dimension is significantly lower than that of the system itself. For this reason, MOR methods
search for the solution of a set of governing equations in a subspace, thereby offering a potential for
significant CPU time reductions [1–8]. The following additional observations are also noteworthy:
 (i) A trajectory associated with a nonlinear static, dynamical, or parametric system can explore
     many regions of the state space. Different portions of such a trajectory can be associated with
     distinct regimes of the underlying physical process or a moving feature such as a discontinuity
     or a front.
(ii) A reduced-order basis (ROB) describing a lower-dimensional subspace often lacks robustness
     with respect to parameter variations [9–13]. This is because the trajectories associated with
     different configurations of the same parametric system tend to localize around different points

      of the state space—for example, the steady states of a dynamical system associated with the

   Both additional observations outlined previously suggest that, in general, a nonlinear trajectory
is more efficiently and better approximated by a set of local subspaces than by a single global
one. Indeed, relying on a single ROB for the purpose of nonlinear MOR could either imply an
unaffordable large set of global basis vectors or one that is too small to span the local behavior
of every portion of the trajectory. For this reason, a global ROB often leads to a poor nonlinear
reduced-order model (ROM) approximation [14].
   Therefore, the dimension of an ROB should ideally be tailored for the purpose of capturing only
the local behavior of a nonlinear or parametric system at a given point of the state space. This leads
to the concept of a local ROB that is further developed in this paper. This concept is particularly
appropriate for the proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) method [15] that constructs an ROB
from snapshots of the system taken at various locations of the state space. A simple aspect of this
concept was already introduced in [16] where ROBs were built and exploited according to a time-
domain partition. Local ROBs were also exploited in [17] in the context of aeroelastic applications
and according to a space-related partition using an error estimator based on the displacement of
the fluid mesh. However, the computational complexity of the error estimator used in [17] scales
with the size of the underlying high-dimensional model (HDM). In this work, the local ROBs are
not linked a priori neither to a time-domain nor to a space-domain partition. Instead, they are built
using an unsupervised learning algorithm that clusters snapshots without any a priori assumption.
More importantly, the local ROBs introduced in this paper are exploited at a computational cost that
scales with the size of the ROM. Furthermore, whenever switching from a previous local ROB to a
new one, the MOR methods presented in [17] and [16] require projecting the current state solution
of the ROM simulation onto the new ROB. This results in the truncation of some components of
the state, and therefore potentially in additional errors. In [18], the authors have shown that repeated
projections of the state solution on consecutive ROBs may also introduce numerical instabilities in
the ROM simulation. In contrast, the nonlinear MOR method proposed in this paper does not require
any projection when switching from one local ROB to another. This is because at each time instance,
the proposed method uses a local ROB to approximate the state increments and not the full state.
   In many cases, the implementation of a nonlinear MOR method based on projection includes at
least one step whose computational complexity scales with the size of the underlying HDM. For this
reason, many nonlinear MOR methods are equipped with a hyper reduction technique that elimi-
nates this caveat at the cost of an additional approximation [19]. Gappy POD [5–7, 20, 21] is one
such technique that achieves the stated objective by constructing an ROB for approximating various
terms arising during the solution of a nonlinear problem. In this case, the same ROB is subsequently
used for interpolating a nonlinear function. The approach proposed in this paper for introducing and
exploiting local ROBs equally applies to hyper reduction. In fact, it is particularly beneficial to this
specific approximation technique as it is well known that constructing an accurate local interpolant
is easier than constructing an equally accurate global one.
   The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the focus is set on discrete
dynamical problems, and some elements of the proposed method for nonlinear MOR based on local
ROBs are introduced within the larger framework of residual minimization and hyper reduction.
Then, the proposed method is fully developed in Sections 3 and 4. Its application to two nonlinear
dynamical systems arising in computational fluid mechanics and fluid/structure/electric interaction
is discussed in Section 5. Finally, conclusions are offered in Section 6.

multivariate function locally than globally. For this reason, it is proposed here to approximate ri and
Wi using local ROB matrices Vrj and VWj , respectively, as follows

4.2. Offline stage: construction of hyper reduced local nonlinear models using Gauss–Newton
with approximated tensor quantities
Gauss–Newton with approximated tensor quantities collects snapshots for the residual and Jacobian
ROBs from preliminary ROM simulations performed offline. The same approach is adopted here

simulations based on ROMs that are constructed using local ROBs as described in Section 3. The
generated snapshots are stored in NV matrices according to which local state ROB is active when the
snapshot is computed. This leads to the construction of NV independent GNAT models following
the numerical procedure presented in [6].

                                           5. APPLICATIONS

The proposed method for nonlinear MOR based on the concept of local ROBs described in
Sections 3 and 4 is illustrated here with two complementary applications: the solution of an aca-
demic Burgers’ problem and the simulation of an Microelectromechanical System (MEMS) device
governed by a fluid-structure-electric interaction. The first application features a moving discontinu-
ity and front. It focuses on demonstrating the ability of the proposed nonlinear MOR to accurately
reproduce HDM results using a much smaller ROM. The second application features parameter
variations. It focuses particularly on demonstrating the predictive capability of the proposed MOR
method—that it, its ability to deliver accurate results using ROBs constructed from data associated
with different parameter values than those used during the ROM simulations. Both applications have
been considered in previous nonlinear model reduction studies [1, 28, 29].
   In both aforementioned applications, the definition of the distance between two state vectors is
based on the 2-norm in Rn . All corresponding simulations are performed with MATLAB running
on Mac Book Pro 2.66 GHz Intel Core 2 Duo, 4GB 1067 MHz DDR3.

                                            6. CONCLUSIONS

A new nonlinear MOR framework based on the concept of local ROBs is introduced. This frame-
work is equally applicable to nonlinear static, dynamic, and parametric problems. Like most MOR
frameworks, it consists of two stages: one that is performed offline and one that is performed
online. In the offline stage, the solution space is partitioned into subregions that characterize the
nonlinear features of the solutions of interest. In practice, this partitioning is performed by com-
puting snapshots of the solution, clustering them according to their relative distances using the
k-means algorithm, computing in each cluster an ROB using, for example, the POD method, iden-
tifying each snapshot cluster with a subregion of the solution space, and assigning to this subregion
the ROB computed in that cluster. In the online stage, the subregion of the solution space containing
the high-dimensional state vector at the previous time step or nonlinear iteration is first identi-
fied. Then, a low-dimensional approximation of the incremental solution is constructed in the affine
space containing the previous state vector and spanned by the local ROB of the identified subre-
gion of the solution space by solving a residual minimization problem. Hence, by construction, this
local framework for nonlinear MOR is well suited for physical problems characterized by differ-
ent regimes, parameter variations, or moving features such as discontinuities and fronts. It equally
accommodates a hyper reduction process that is usually necessary for achieving practical speedups.
The results obtained for the solution of a moving shock problem and those obtained for the simula-
tion of a fluid-structure-electric interaction problem associated with an MEMS device reveal that, for
the same desired accuracy, the dimension of each local affine subspace in which a low-order approx-
imation of the incremental solution can be found is in practice much smaller than the dimension of
the global affine subspace in which the entire trajectory of the state vector lies or can be well approx-
imated. They also reveal that in the worst case, the dimension of the union of all affine subspaces in
which the incremental solutions lie is of the same order of that of the global affine subspace in which
the entire trajectory of the state vector lies or can be well approximated. Hence, these results show
that, in general, the local framework proposed in this paper for nonlinear MOR is more accurate,
computationally more efficient, and therefore superior to its standard global counterpart.
