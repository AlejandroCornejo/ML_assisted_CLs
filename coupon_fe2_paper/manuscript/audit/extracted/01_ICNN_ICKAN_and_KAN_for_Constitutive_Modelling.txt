ICNN, ICKAN, and KAN for Constitutive Modelling
Executive summary
The literature splits into three distinct but partially converging lines. The ﬁrst is the general architectural
line: ICNNs begin with Amos, Xu, and Kolter’s 2017 ICML paper, which introduced neural networks that are
convex in designated inputs and made convex optimization part of inference; KANs begin with Liu et al.’s
2024 work, later appearing in ICLR 2025, which moved learnable nonlinearities from nodes to edges and
made symbolic inspection central; ICKANs, in the strict architectural sense, appear as a published
mathematical construction in 2026 through Deschatre and Warin’s SIAM Journal on Scientiﬁc Computing
paper, which embeds ICNN structure inside KAN parameterizations. 1


The second line is the constitutive-modelling line. Here, the center of gravity is not generic machine
learning venues but mechanics journals, especially Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids, Computer
Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, Journal of Computational Physics, and International Journal for
Numerical Methods in Engineering. In this literature, ICNN-like convexity constraints are not decorative. They
are used to enforce polyconvexity, objectivity, admissible stress responses, and ﬁnite element compatibility.
The highest-impact papers in this line are Klein et al. on polyconvex anisotropic hyperelasticity, As’ad, Avery,
and Farhat on mechanics-informed constitutive ANNs, Linden et al. on enforcing physics in hyperelasticity,
and the broader CANN line associated with Linka, Kuhl, Cyron, and coauthors. 2


The third line is the recent convergence of KAN-style interpretability with constitutive modeling. That
convergence is real, but still narrow. The clearest peer-reviewed constitutive paper in this category is
Thakolkaran et al.’s 2025 CMAME article, which introduces monotonic ICKANs for polyconvex hyperelastic
constitutive laws and couples them to unsupervised training from full-ﬁeld strain data and limited force
measurements. Beyond that, the KAN constitutive literature is still immature, with 2026 work on strain-
limiting elasticity and inelastic iCKANs remaining at preprint stage rather than already stabilized in top
mechanics journals. 3


The strongest substantive conclusion is therefore not that KANs have displaced ICNNs in constitutive
modeling. They have not. The constitutive literature remains dominated by ICNN-adjacent, CANN, and
PANN formulations, mostly in hyperelasticity. KANs are promising because they may relax the usual trade-
oﬀ between interpretability and admissibility, but the evidence base is still thin, dataset-speciﬁc, and
concentrated on isotropic or tightly structured hyperelastic settings. Claims of a broad constitutive
breakthrough would be premature. 4

Analytical synthesis of the ﬁeld
The architectural story begins with ICNNs. Amos et al. made convexity an architectural property rather than
an ex post regularizer. That move had immediate value in optimization-heavy domains and later became
attractive in constitutive modeling because polyconvex or convex free energies are mechanically safer than
unconstrained black-box regressors. However, the original ICNN paper is not about materials, and
constitutive adoption required substantial reinterpretation of what “input convexity” should be convex in. In
mechanics, convexity in the wrong variables is useless or even misleading. 9


The constitutive uptake of ICNN ideas was led most clearly by papers such as Klein et al. 2022 and Linden et
al. 2023. Klein et al. showed how to impose polyconvexity for anisotropic hyperelasticity using either
invariant-based or deformation-gradient/cofactor/determinant formulations, and they calibrated against
demanding metamaterial data that include instabilities. Linden et al. then systematized the admissibility
checklist and argued, correctly, that the hard part is not ﬁtting stress data but satisfying all constitutive
conditions simultaneously while remaining usable in ﬁnite element simulations. This is one reason that the

The As’ad and Farhat line is diﬀerent in emphasis. It is less tied to the exact ICNN label, but very much tied
to the same scientiﬁc objective: embed mechanics directly into architecture so that the learned constitutive
law is robust, simulation-ready, and less data-hungry. Their 2022 IJNME paper is now highly cited and
remains one of the clearest demonstrations that hard-wiring objectivity, consistency, dynamic stability, and
material stability into the network pays oﬀ practically, including in multiscale parachute simulation. Their
later work extends the same philosophy to nonlinear viscoelasticity and then to staggered multiscale
homogenization of woven fabrics. This body of work is directly relevant even though it is not branded as
ICNN or KAN. 11


KANs changed the interpretability discussion, but not yet the constitutive state of the art. Liu et al.’s KAN
paper and the later PRX “KAN 2.0” paper made KANs visible by promising compact models, symbolic
extraction, and scientiﬁc discovery workﬂows. That visibility spilled into mechanics quickly, but constitutive
mechanics is not an easy test bed. A constitutive model is not just a smooth scalar regressor. It must usually
satisfy invariance, stress consistency, energy normalization, and numerical stability under FE diﬀerentiation.
As a result, the constitutive KAN line has had to re-import ideas from ICNN and hyperelasticity rather than
replacing them. Thakolkaran et al. 2025 is the decisive example: its contribution is not “use a KAN,” but
“make KAN splines monotonic and input-convex so that physically admissible polyconvex hyperelastic laws
become learnable and interpretable.” 12


There is also a sharp asymmetry in application maturity. For ICNN-adjacent methods, there is already a
substantial peer-reviewed hyperelastic literature and a growing viscoelastic/inelastic one. For KAN/ICKAN
methods, the peer-reviewed constitutive literature is much thinner and mainly hyperelastic. The more
ambitious inelastic KAN papers I found in 2026 remain preprints. That does not make them irrelevant, but it
does make any broad generalization unwise. The evidence base is currently too recent, too paper-speciﬁc,
and too benchmark-fragmented. 13


A second asymmetry concerns anisotropy. Many papers speak as if “anisotropy” were solved once invariants
or structure tensors are added. That is overstated. Klein et al. use named symmetry settings or
symmetrization strategies; Kalina et al. extend the scope using generalized structure tensors and
simultaneous calibration of the tensors themselves; but the combination of exact Ball-polyconvexity with
broad, group-free anisotropy remains limited. The uploaded memorandum identiﬁes exactly this gap, and
that diagnosis matches the published literature. 14

Learning constitutive relations from indirect observations using deep neural networks
Authors: Daniel Z. Huang, Kailai Xu, Charbel Farhat, Eric Darve. Year: 2020. Venue: Journal of Computational
Physics. DOI: 10.1016/j.jcp.2020.109491. Abstract summary: Learns coarse-grained constitutive laws from
indirect observation data rather than direct local stress-strain pairs, combining ﬁnite elements, automatic
diﬀerentiation, and neural approximators. The paper includes convergence/error analysis and uncertainty
quantiﬁcation, and demonstrates the method on a multiscale ﬁber-reinforced plate and a nonlinear
membrane problem. Why it matters here: this is the earliest high-impact Farhat-group paper squarely in
learned constitutive modeling, and it foreshadows the group’s later mechanics-informed ANN line.
Constitutive modelling orientation: direct. Citation count: publisher page reports 150 cited-by articles.

A mechanics-informed artiﬁcial neural network approach in data-driven constitutive modeling
Authors: Faisal As’ad, Philip Avery, Charbel Farhat. Year: 2022. Venue: International Journal for Numerical
Methods in Engineering. DOI: 10.1002/nme.6957. Abstract summary: Proposes a structure-preserving
constitutive ANN that embeds objectivity, rigid-body-mode consistency, dynamic stability, and material
stability in the architecture. The paper argues and demonstrates that such constraints improve robustness
to noise and extrapolation, and it shows ﬁnite element applications including acceleration of multiscale
simulation for parachute inﬂation dynamics. Why it matters here: this is the most important exact match

Polyconvex anisotropic hyperelasticity with neural networks
Authors: Dominik K. Klein, Mauricio Fernández, Robert J. Martin, Patrizio Neﬀ, Oliver Weeger. Year: 2022.
Venue: Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids. DOI: 10.1016/j.jmps.2021.104703. Abstract summary:
Develops two machine-learning constitutive models for ﬁnite deformations that are hyperelastic,
anisotropic, and polyconvex by construction. One uses polyconvex anisotropic objective invariants; the
other works directly with deformation-gradient, cofactor, and determinant inputs plus symmetrization and
augmentation. The benchmark data come from challenging cubic-lattice metamaterial simulations with
ﬁnite deformations and instabilities. Why it matters here: this is arguably the seminal peer-reviewed ICNN-
style hyperelastic constitutive paper in top-tier mechanics. Constitutive modelling orientation: direct.
Citation count: publisher page reports 191 cited-by articles. 19

Neural networks meet hyperelasticity: A guide to enforcing physics
Authors: Lennart Linden, Dominik K. Klein, Karl A. Kalina, Jörg Brummund, Oliver Weeger, Markus Kästner.
Year: 2023. Venue: Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids. DOI: 10.1016/j.jmps.2023.105363. Abstract

A mechanics-informed deep learning framework for data-driven nonlinear viscoelasticity
Authors: Faisal As’ad, Charbel Farhat. Year: 2023. Venue: Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and

Year: 2025. Venue: Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering. DOI: 10.1016/j.cma.

A hard comparison across papers is weaker than many abstracts imply. The datasets, supervision levels,
constitutive assumptions, and metrics are not standardized. Some papers optimize stress errors from local
material tests, others ﬁt full-ﬁeld measurements plus global forces, others compare free-energy landscapes
or tangent consistency, and still others emphasize FE robustness. The absence of a mature, peer-reviewed
benchmark suite speciﬁc to admissible constitutive architectures remains a methodological weakness in the
ﬁeld. The best explicit cross-family comparison I found is still the 2023 preprint benchmarking CANN, ICNN,
and NODE on rubber and skin data, which is useful but not yet a deﬁnitive standard. 31

This timeline makes one fact obvious. The constitutive community adopted convex and physics-constrained
network design years before KANs arrived, and KAN-based constitutive modeling is therefore a second-
wave development grafted onto an already sophisticated admissibility literature. Any reading of the ﬁeld
that starts with KANs will miss most of the mechanics content. 32


Gaps and promising research directions
The largest gap is beyond-hyperelastic constitutive breadth. ICNN and ICKAN methods are strongest
where a convex free-energy construction gives immediate leverage, namely hyperelasticity. Once one
enters viscoelasticity, plasticity, damage, or coupled thermo-chemo-mechanical behavior, the architectural
problem becomes much harder because one must learn not only an admissible stored energy but also
dissipation, history evolution, and internal-variable structure. The As’ad viscoelastic line, the vCANN line,
and the 2026 iCKAN preprints all point in this direction, but the published KAN-speciﬁc literature has not yet
established a mature solution. 33


A second gap is anisotropy without over-prescription. Most constitutive architectures still lean on named
symmetry classes, ﬁxed invariant sets, or learned structure tensors. That is scientiﬁcally useful, but it is not
the same as learning broad anisotropy without hard commitment to a material symmetry group. The
uploaded memorandum is right to treat this as unresolved, and the published mechanics literature
supports that assessment. Kalina et al. 2025 is a major step, but not the endpoint. 34


A third gap is benchmark quality. Reported metrics vary across RMSE in stress space, free-energy accuracy,
tangent consistency, FE convergence, extrapolation quality, and qualitative agreement under unseen




                                                                                                       10
loading paths. Some papers train on direct material data, others on homogenized synthetic data, others on
indirect observation data, and Thakolkaran et al. 2025 use unsupervised full-ﬁeld-plus-force training. That
diversity is scientiﬁcally interesting, but it prevents clean leaderboard-style claims. A rigorous benchmark
suite would need common datasets, constitutive admissibility checks, FE deployment tests, and uncertainty
reporting. At present, that infrastructure barely exists. 35


A fourth gap is symbolic extraction that remains mechanically faithful. KANs are attractive because they
promise readable formulas. But symbolic readability is not enough if the extracted law loses objectivity,
normalization, or tangential behavior needed for FE solvers. KAN 2.0 and the constitutive ICKAN paper both
emphasize symbolic discovery, yet the ﬁeld still lacks strong, standardized validation of whether the
extracted symbolic forms preserve the numerical advantages of the parent admissible model. This is an
area where KANs could genuinely outperform ICNN-like black-box convex nets, but the burden of proof is
still unmet. 36


The most promising research direction is therefore not “replace ICNNs with KANs.” It is hybrid admissible
symbolic constitutive learning: keep the exact mechanics constraints developed in ICNN/PANN/CANN
traditions, but use KAN/ICKAN parameterizations where they genuinely improve compactness,
identiﬁability, and symbolic extraction. The recent literature is already moving there. The danger is that the
KAN hype cycle outruns the mechanics. The opportunity is that, if handled carefully, KAN-style
parameterizations may ﬁnally make constitutive law discovery both interpretable and simulation-grade. 37
