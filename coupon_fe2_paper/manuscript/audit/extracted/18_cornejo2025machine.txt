HIGH LIGHTS

∙ Finite Element Method, i.e. Micro and Macro Scale modelling and its posterior combination via Multi scale approaches based on the homogenization theory and
  the representative volume element concept.
∙ Machine Learning techniques to develop a novel homogenization strategy for the in-plane analysis of masonry structures.
∙ Application to the in-plane homogenization of a Flemish bond masonry wall. Evaluation examples based on the simulation of shear and compression tests show the
  accuracy of the method when compared with sophisticated micro modeling of the entire structure.

Composite materials                                         nization strategy for the in-plane analysis of masonry structures. This strategy involves automatically calibrating
                                                            a continuum nonlinear damage constitutive law and an appropriate yield criteria using relevant data derived
                                                            from Micro-scale analysis. The optimization process not only enhances material parameters but also refines yield
                                                            criteria and damage evolution laws to better align with existing data.
                                                                To achieve this, a virtual laboratory is created to conduct micro-model simulations that account for the indi-

                                                            amples, including simulations of shear and compression tests, demonstrate the method’s accuracy compared to
                                                            micro modeling of the entire structure.

                                                                                           5. Conclusions and future work

                                                                                               This research has presented the definition of a machine learning
                                                                                           based technique capable of homogenizing a composite material like ma-
                                                                                           sonry. The smeared properties, within the framework of a macro mod-
                                                                                           elling approach, are presented in a single damage constitutive model.
                                                                                           In comparison to conventional homogenization techniques, the novel
                                                                                           procedure disconnects micro and macro analysis scales while homoge-
                                                                                           nizing the material, with significant benefits in terms of computational
                                                                                           efficiency.
                                                                                               Setting up a virtual laboratory allows one to produce numerical re-
                                                                                           sults that can be utilized for big data analysis tools, such as machine
                                                                                           learning or artificial intelligence. This approach clearly promotes the
                                                                                           idea of assessing the mechanical properties of materials by using directly
                                                                                           processed data. In this regard, a structural simulation performed in the
