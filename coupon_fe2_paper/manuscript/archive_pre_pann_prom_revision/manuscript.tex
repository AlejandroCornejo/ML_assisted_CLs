\begin{abstract}
Finite-strain computational homogenization couples constitutive accuracy to the repeated solution of microscopic equilibrium problems. This work investigates two complementary ways to reduce that cost: projection-based hyperreduced micromechanics and learned macroscopic constitutive energies. For the latter, paired directional features with trainable orientations, powers, and determinant exponents are coupled to either an input-convex neural network (ICNN) or an input-convex spline network (ICKAN). The features preserve objectivity and two-dimensional polyconvexity while admitting an isotropic reference derivative, so that affine determinant normalization separates the stress-free reference condition from volumetric stiffness. A separate sufficient lower bound addresses nonnegative energy for the selected parameters. The microscopic alternatives comprise a linear ECM-based HPROM, an HPROM--ANN with primary/secondary latent-space closure and adaptive cubature, and a direct decoder variant without microscopic equilibrium iterations. All use genuine reduced meshes. A periodic cell containing a rotated elliptical void tests approximation and mechanical consistency: the selected polyconvex models attain approximately $0.65\%$ held-out stress error, while a closed strain cycle reveals a nonconservative defect in direct stress regression. A common tensile-coupon calculation then measures structural error and online cost against nested FOM--FE$^2$. The iterative HPROM--ANN reduces the microscopic equilibrium dimension from 39 to three and is $2.23$ times faster than the optimized linear HPROM, with a $0.052\%$ displacement error. ICNN and ICKAN yield displacement errors below $0.43\%$ in 1.73 and 3.67 seconds under the reported thread configurations. These results establish distinct accuracy--cost operating points and demonstrate that accurate polyconvex learning is compatible with this anisotropic homogenized response. Constitutive guarantees, sampled extrapolation behavior, and nominal finite element convergence are assessed separately.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} computational homogenization; hyperelasticity; polyconvex neural networks; input-convex splines; empirical cubature; nonlinear model reduction.
