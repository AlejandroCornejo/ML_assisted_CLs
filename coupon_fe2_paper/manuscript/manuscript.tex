\begin{abstract}
Approximating the effective anisotropic hyperelastic response of a representative volume element (RVE) requires constitutive expressiveness, mechanical consistency, and computational efficiency. Non-intrusive neural surrogates provide direct constitutive evaluations, whereas intrusive projection-based reduced-order models (PROMs) approximate the microscopic state and equilibrium equations. This work develops a polyconvex physics-augmented neural network (PANN) construction based on paired directional features with trainable orientations, stretch powers, and determinant exponents. The features are convex in independent deformation minors and are composed with convex, componentwise nondecreasing cores realized as an input-convex neural network (ICNN) or an input-convex Kolmogorov--Arnold network (ICKAN). Their isotropic reference derivatives enable affine-determinant stress normalization without compromising polyconvexity. The complete energies satisfy objectivity, an exactly stress-free reference, two-dimensional polyconvexity, and prescribed growth conditions by construction; global nonnegative energy is assessed through a separate sufficient bound for the trained parameters. Projection-based surrogates with hyperreduction provide a complementary benchmark for structural accuracy and online cost. On a periodic cell with a rotated elliptical void, the selected polyconvex models attain approximately $0.65\%$ held-out stress error. A tensile-coupon application illustrates structural deployment against a full-order FE$^2$ reference. The ICNN and ICKAN give displacement errors below $0.43\%$; the iterative nonlinear reduced model gives $0.052\%$ error and its implemented workflow is $2.23$ times faster than the optimized affine reduced model under the reported configurations. These results demonstrate the practical use of the constrained constitutive representation and quantify its accuracy--cost trade-off against reduced microscopic solvers.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} computational homogenization; hyperelasticity; physics-augmented neural networks; polyconvexity; projection-based reduced-order models; empirical cubature.
