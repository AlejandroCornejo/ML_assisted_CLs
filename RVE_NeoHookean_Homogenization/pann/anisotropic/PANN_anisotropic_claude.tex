\begin{abstract}
Physics-augmented neural networks (PANNs) for hyperelastic constitutive
modelling are commonly held to a six-point admissibility checklist:
thermodynamic consistency, objectivity, tensor symmetry, a stress-free
reference state, polyconvexity, and a growth
condition~\cite{Klein2022,Ball1977}. Existing constructions achieve all
six only for a material with a named symmetry group, or give up one
property to handle general anisotropy; whether this trade-off is
fundamental is an explicit open problem in the recent literature. This
paper resolves it for a two-dimensional anisotropic representative
volume element (RVE): an energy-based PANN, built from an input-convex
neural network (ICNN) acting on balanced directional invariants of
$\bm F$ and $\Cof\bm F$, satisfies all six properties exactly and by
construction, with closed-form proofs rather than training penalties,
including an explicit polyconvexity construction in the sense of
Ball~\cite{Ball1977}. The same proofs transfer verbatim to a second
convex core, an input-convex Kolmogorov--Arnold network (ICKAN).
Benchmarked on a test trajectory against a projection-based
hyper-reduced-order model in both its Newton-corrected (HPROM--ANN) and
direct (D-HPROM--ANN) operating modes, and against a direct regression
baseline, the admissibility certificate proves necessary in practice:
the uncertified regression baseline matches or exceeds every certified
model's test-trajectory accuracy while directly violating thermodynamic
consistency, and a matched uncertified flexible PANN fails a rank-one
convexity check on $7\%$ of sampled deformation states despite equally
strong accuracy. Deployed as the macroscopic material law of an
unrelated structural benchmark, Cook's membrane, this same distinction
determines whether the structural solve itself succeeds: the certified
models converge in three Newton iterations at every ramped load step,
while the uncertified flexible PANN fails to converge at the very first
step -- a real structural consequence of the missing certificate, even
though the identical rank-one check finds no violation at the specific
states this particular problem visits. Nesting the same reduced-order surrogates, and, once, the
true, non-reduced RVE itself, as the actual microscale solver at every
Gauss point -- a genuine FE$^2$ computation, not a direct deployment --
confirms this robustness result, and, by comparing every model directly
against the true multiscale answer for the first time rather than only
against each other, exposes a small-strain accuracy gap in the
certified cores that the test-trajectory check misses; one additional,
cheap loss term, matching each model's own tangent stiffness at zero
strain to the RVE's true linear-elastic limit, corrects most of it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Multiscale (FE$^2$-type) computational homogenization
resolves each macroscopic material point by solving a nonlinear boundary
value problem on a representative volume element (RVE), so that the
macroscopic constitutive response is not postulated but computed from the
microstructure directly. This is expensive: every macroscopic Gauss point,
at every load step and every Newton iteration, requires its own converged
microscale solve. Two largely separate lines of work have grown up around
reducing this cost, and they sit on opposite sides of the classic
\emph{non-intrusive}/\emph{intrusive} divide in reduced-order and
surrogate modelling.

The non-intrusive route treats the microscale solve as a black
box: precompute enough RVE solutions offline, then learn a direct
surrogate for the homogenized constitutive law itself, so that no
microscale solve is needed online at all. Early versions of this idea
learn a bare regression map from strain to stress; the physics-augmented
neural network (PANN) literature that has grown from
it~\cite{Klein2022,Linden2023,Asad2022} instead builds physical structure
into the learned energy potential itself: thermodynamic consistency,
objectivity, material-frame symmetry, a stress-free reference state,
polyconvexity, and a growth condition, so that the surrogate is
trustworthy by construction, not merely accurate where it happens to have
been trained.

The intrusive route, by contrast, keeps the RVE's finite element
structure and governing equations explicit, and instead compresses the
linear algebra: a projection-based reduced-order model (PROM) restricts
the unknown displacement field to a low-dimensional subspace identified
by proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) of precomputed snapshots,
tracing back to the reduced-model multiscale method of Yvonnet and
He~\cite{Yvonnet2007} and developed since for nonlinear multiscale
structural problems~\cite{Zahr2017,He2020,Fritzen2016,Hernandez2014,Hernandez2020}.
A single linear subspace, however, is generally not enough to reach high
accuracy for a genuinely nonlinear problem (the Kolmogorov $n$-width
barrier), which more recent work addresses by correcting the subspace
with a trained nonlinear-manifold map (the PROM-ANN/HPROM-ANN framework
of Barnett, Farhat and Maday~\cite{Barnett2022,Barnett2023}) and by
replacing the full Gauss-point residual assembly with a sparse, learned
cubature rule via the empirical cubature method (ECM) and its
manifold-adaptive-weight extension
(MAW--ECM)~\cite{Hernandez2017,HernandezMAWECM}. Throughout this route,
the true microscale equilibrium equations are still being solved, just
in compressed and hyperreduced form: the RVE's own physics is never
bypassed.

Both routes are active research areas in their own right, and both are
used in this paper, not as competitors but as two independent,
non-interchangeable sources of ground truth and comparison for the same
RVE, evaluated on the same test load path: a rare case of the
non-intrusive and intrusive philosophies being confronted with exactly
the same problem rather than argued for in isolation.

For the non-intrusive, PANN route specifically, a recurring difficulty
motivates the deeper focus of this paper. Physics-augmented neural constitutive models are commonly held to an
admissibility checklist: (C1) thermodynamic consistency, (C2)
objectivity, (C3) tensor symmetry, (C4) a stress-free reference state, (C5)
polyconvexity, and (C6) a growth condition. Table~\ref{tab:literature}
checks eight directly comparable constructions against this list, and no
two broad strategies in the current literature yet achieve all six
properties together for a general anisotropic material.

The first keeps polyconvexity exact but ties it to a named symmetry
group: Klein et al.~\cite{Klein2022} and Linden et
al.~\cite{Linden2023} give exact, architecturally-certified constructions
for isotropic and transversely isotropic materials via a
structural-tensor integrity basis, recently widened to cubic and
tetragonal symmetry classes~\cite{Klein2026}, but Linden et al.\ state
explicitly that a complete, polyconvexity-compatible invariant set ``may
not exist for some symmetry groups $\dots$ it might be possible that
there does not exist a set of invariants to describe the anisotropy of
materials with arbitrary microstructures,'' and flag a general,
symmetry-agnostic construction as an open problem. The second keeps a
more flexible or general anisotropy representation but gives up
something else: Kalina et al.~\cite{Kalina2024} learn the structural
tensor's own parameters jointly with the network, more flexible than a
fixed structural tensor, but explicitly decline polyconvexity, ``too
restrictive, especially for multiscale modeling''; As'ad, Avery and
Farhat~\cite{Asad2022} instead substitute a strictly stronger condition,
convexity of $W$ in $\bm E$ (equivalent to a global SPD tangent), which
Section~\ref{sec:c5-wrong} shows is measurably false for this RVE's own
ground truth. A third cluster gets polyconvexity right, via the same
additive, block-decoupled sufficient condition used here, but restricts
anisotropy to at most two fixed fiber directions and rarely exercises the
growth condition (C6) in practice, since the materials studied are
(nearly) incompressible~\cite{Linka2023,Tac2023,Thakolkaran2025}.
Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction} onward discusses all eight works
against every checklist item in the detail Table~\ref{tab:literature}
only summarizes.

Whether this trade-off is fundamental, or an artifact of how the
anisotropy has been encoded so far, is the central question this
paper answers for a concrete, fully worked $2$D RVE: a Neo-Hookean
solid with a hole, admitting no assumed material symmetry.

Answering that question carefully also requires knowing, precisely, what
``accurate'' does and does not certify. A model can match or exceed a
certified model's test-trajectory accuracy while silently violating thermodynamic
consistency or losing rank-one convexity at ordinary deformation states;
this is not a hypothetical concern but something demonstrated directly on
this project's own data in Sections~\ref{sec:c1-regression}
and~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex}. This is also why the paper evaluates six
model variants side by side on the identical test path,
spanning a genuine spectrum of embedded physical structure: a pure
regression surrogate with no potential at all, a free (uncertified) energy
surrogate, two independently certified polyconvex cores (ICNN and ICKAN),
and the two operating modes of HPROM--ANN, a projection-based model that
satisfies every admissibility property automatically because it solves
(a hyperreduced form of) the true microscale equilibrium equations rather
than learning a constitutive law at all.

\paragraph{Contributions.} This paper's own contributions are
specifically:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item A closed-form construction of a PANN for a general, group-free
 anisotropic hyperelastic material that satisfies (C1)--(C6) exactly, by
 construction, including exact polyconvexity in Ball's sense
 (Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction}), to our knowledge the first
 construction to combine exact objectivity, an exact stress-free reference
 state, and exact polyconvexity without assuming or exploiting any named
 material symmetry class (Section~\ref{sec:related-gap}). We also verify
 the correct necessary consequence of polyconvexity (rank-one convexity)
 numerically, and show that a strictly stronger, globally SPD-tangent
 condition used elsewhere in the literature for a different material is
 not required by polyconvexity and is, for this RVE's own ground truth,
 false (Section~\ref{sec:c5-wrong}).
 \item A direct, quantitative demonstration that test-trajectory accuracy alone
 cannot certify admissibility: a pure-regression baseline with no energy
 potential is the \emph{most} accurate of all six model variants while
 directly violating (C1) (Section~\ref{sec:c1-regression}), and an
 otherwise-identical uncertified free model matches the certified models'
 accuracy while failing a rank-one convexity check on $7\%$ of sampled
 states (Section~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex}).
 \item Verification that the same certificate transfers, proof-for-proof,
 to a second convex core (an input-convex Kolmogorov--Arnold network,

 al.~\cite{Asad2022} and Klein et al.~\cite{Klein2022}, that the

 including the projection-based ROM/HPROM surrogates of
 Section~\ref{sec:rom-comparison} nested as the actual microscale solver

 itself nested the same way, is deployed as the macroscopic material law

\section{Background: the representative volume element and its full-order model}
\label{sec:background}
This section fixes notation and gives a self-contained, high-level account
of the RVE and its full-order model (FOM),
which every model in this paper (the PANN as well as the projection-based
and hyper-reduced comparison models of Section~\ref{sec:rom-comparison})
is ultimately trained on, evaluated against, or built from. None of the
constructions in this section are a contribution of this work; they are
established tools, cited to their sources, applied here to a specific RVE.

\subsection{The representative volume element and its full-order model}
\label{sec:background-fom}
The RVE is a square unit cell with a central circular hole, Fig.~\ref{fig:rve-geometry},
discretized by a standard displacement-based finite element mesh and
governed by a single homogeneous, isotropic, compressible Neo-Hookean solid
($E=1.628\,\mathrm{GPa}$, $\nu=0.4$). A macroscopic strain
$\bm\varepsilon=(E_{11},E_{22},\gamma_{12})$ is imposed through an affine Dirichlet
condition on the outer boundary nodes (Fig.~\ref{fig:rve-geometry}, red),
while the remaining boundary and interior nodes, including the nodes on
the hole, are left free. We write $\bm u$ for the full nodal
displacement field over every node of the mesh (this is what is plotted
in Fig.~\ref{fig:displacement-field}), partitioned into its Dirichlet and
free parts as $\bm u=(\bm u_d,\bm u_f)$: on the Dirichlet boundary,
$\bm u_d$ is prescribed exactly, node by node, by the affine
macro-strain map determined by $\bm\varepsilon$: affine in the
reference-configuration position $\bm X$ of each Dirichlet node for a
\emph{fixed} $\bm\varepsilon$, but \emph{not} a linear function of
$\bm\varepsilon$ itself, since (made explicit below) it is built from
the deformation gradient $\bm F(\bm\varepsilon)$, a nonlinear
(matrix-square-root) function of the three components
$E_{11},E_{22},\gamma_{12}$, while $\bm u_f\in\mathbb R^{n_L}$, the
free-node values, are unknown. The
finite element (Galerkin) discretization of
the governing balance of linear momentum reduces, in the quasi-static
setting used here (no inertia, no explicit time dependence: the
test load path is a pseudo-time sequence of equilibrium states,
not a genuine initial-value problem), to the nodal equilibrium residual
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fom-residual}
 \bm r(\bm u_f;\bm\varepsilon) := \sum_{e=1}^{n_{\rm el}}\bm L_e^T\left(\sum_{g=1}^{n_{\rm gp}^{(e)}}\bm B_{e,g}^T\,\bm S_{e,g}(\bm u_f;\bm\varepsilon)\,w_{e,g}\right)=\bm 0,
\end{equation}
where $\bm L_e$ is the Boolean element-assembly operator, $\bm B_{e,g}$ is
the element strain-displacement operator, $\bm S_{e,g}$ is the second
Piola--Kirchhoff stress at Gauss point $g$ of element $e$ (evaluated from
the true microscopic Neo-Hookean law, not a surrogate, and depending on
$\bm u_f$ and the fixed, prescribed $\bm u_d(\bm\varepsilon)$ together), and
$w_{e,g}$ is the
corresponding FE quadrature weight (parent-domain weight times the
isoparametric Jacobian determinant). Equation~\eqref{eq:fom-residual} is
solved by Newton--Raphson for $\bm u_f$ at every prescribed $\bm\varepsilon$ along a
load path; this is the FOM, and it is what generates every training
snapshot and every test-trajectory ground-truth number (energy, stress, and their
components) used elsewhere in this paper. Throughout this paper,
$\bm\varepsilon$ denotes this prescribed, exact boundary strain: the
deformation gradient $\bm F=\bm U=\sqrt{2\bm E(\bm\varepsilon)+\bm I}$
(the unique symmetric, rotation-free square root) is used to build the
affine map $\bm u_d(\bm X)=(\bm F-\bm I)\bm X$ directly, so the outer
boundary's own Green-Lagrange strain equals $\bm\varepsilon$ exactly, by
construction, unlike the homogenized macro-\emph{stress}, which is a
computed output with more than one admissible definition. For $\bm
u_d$ to be well-defined at all, the prescribed $\bm\varepsilon$ must
satisfy $\bm C(\bm\varepsilon):=\bm I+2\bm E(\bm\varepsilon)\succ0$, so
that a real, symmetric square root $\bm F$ exists; the implementation
enforces this explicitly (an out-of-range $\bm\varepsilon$ raises an
error rather than silently returning a spurious $\bm F$), and every
trajectory actually used for training or testing (Fig.~\ref{fig:training-trajectories})
stays at moderate strain magnitudes that satisfy it comfortably, far
from this boundary. The
homogenized macro-stress
reported throughout is \emph{not} the naive volume average of
$\{\bm S_{e,g}\}$ (see the distinction drawn in
Section~\ref{sec:results}), but the reaction-force-based, exactly
energy-conjugate quantity described there; a volume-averaged
homogenized \emph{strain} of the converged solution can likewise be
computed and differs measurably from $\bm\varepsilon$ for this
mixed Dirichlet/free RVE (the free hole boundary is not affine-constrained,
so the classical boundary-average identity that would equate the two
does not apply), but this paper does not use that quantity anywhere:
$\bm\varepsilon$, being the independent (prescribed) variable rather
than a computed output, requires no such choice.

The outer-boundary condition used here is affine Dirichlet, not
periodic. Periodic boundary conditions are the more common convention
in idealized unit-cell homogenization precisely because, under them,
the Hill--Mandel macro-homogeneity condition holds automatically at the
level of the deformation gradient, with no further argument needed
(Appendix~\ref{app:periodicity} states the exact identity, and why it
does not extend to the strain $\bm E$ without an added qualification).
Neither this identity nor its stress counterpart is automatic for a
mixed Dirichlet/free RVE like this one, which is exactly why the
applied $\bm\varepsilon$ and the volume-averaged, computed strain/stress
quantities are kept explicitly distinct above and in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. The reaction-force
procedure used there does not rely on periodicity anywhere in its
derivation: it follows from the envelope theorem at any converged
equilibrium state, for any boundary-condition type, so the same
machinery applies unchanged to RVEs for which a periodic idealization
is not assumed or not available, such as a finite specimen, an RVE
adjacent to a free edge, or a boundary condition dictated by an
experimental protocol rather than by periodic tiling.

\subsection{The offline data-generation campaign: training trajectories and the test path}
\label{sec:data-campaign}
Every model compared in this paper, the four PANN tiers of
Section~\ref{sec:c1-regression} as well as the POD basis, the decoder
$\mathcal N$, and the ECM/MAW--ECM cubature rules behind HPROM--ANN
(Section~\ref{sec:rom-comparison}), is built from the same underlying
dataset: converged FOM solves of \eqref{eq:fom-residual} along
prescribed macro-strain paths $\bm\varepsilon(t)=(E_{11}(t),E_{22}(t),\gamma_{12}(t))$,
each solved state contributing one labeled triple (applied strain,
energy-conjugate stress, stored energy) to the dataset. This section
describes, once and explicitly, how those paths were chosen and how
large the resulting dataset is; Section~\ref{sec:training} states how it
is then split and used to train each model.

Figure~\ref{fig:training-trajectories} shows the full training domain:
ten trajectories forming five tension/compression pairs, one pair per
swept shear level $\gamma_{12}\in\{0,\pm0.05,\pm0.1\}$, the two members
of a pair sharing the same shear target but opposite normal-strain
branches. Every
trajectory is genuinely three-dimensional, not a single planar slice: it
first ramps $\gamma_{12}$ from $0$ to its target value at
$E_{11}=E_{22}=0$, then sweeps $(E_{11},E_{22})$ in a boustrophedon
(zig-zag) pattern within that fixed-shear plane, at a fixed row spacing
shared between both branches of a pair, so the sampled states cover an
\emph{area} of the $(E_{11},E_{22})$ plane rather than a single diagonal
ramp. The tension branch of each pair sweeps the larger range
$E_{22}\in[0,2]$; the compression branch sweeps the physically much
shorter range $E_{22}\in[-0.1,0]$ before the hole would close on
itself. Sampled at the same fixed row spacing, this asymmetric range is
also why each tension branch contributes roughly three times as many
solved states as its paired compression branch ($16\,802$--$16\,842$ vs.\
$5\,042$--$5\,082$ states per trajectory). Summed over all ten
trajectories, the FOM of \eqref{eq:fom-residual} is solved at
$109\,460$ distinct macro-strain states, the number reused directly in
Section~\ref{sec:training}.

The test trajectory is a different matter. Every accuracy
number reported for every model in this paper (Table~\ref{tab:master}
onward) is
evaluated on a single, separate, $1\,151$-state trajectory that is never
included in any training set, referred to throughout simply as the
test trajectory. Unlike the ten training trajectories, each of
which holds $\gamma_{12}$ fixed while sweeping $(E_{11},E_{22})$, the
test path mixes all three strain components together in a single
loading-then-unloading cycle that starts and ends at the origin,

\section{The admissibility checklist}
\label{sec:checklist}
This paper organizes six requirements for an admissible hyperelastic
surrogate $W$, with $\bm\sigma=\partial W/\partial\bm\varepsilon$, into a
single checklist for clarity of exposition. Each requirement is
individually well established and is discussed with its own citation in
the section devoted to it below; the six-item grouping itself is this
paper's own organizing device, not a claim that this exact
enumeration is issued by some external authority:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[\textbf{(C1)}] \textbf{Thermodynamic consistency} (reduced
 Clausius--Duhem inequality): $\bm\sigma^T\dot{\bm\varepsilon}-\dot W\geq0$.
 \item[\textbf{(C2)}] \textbf{Objectivity}: $W(\bm Q\bm F)=W(\bm F)$ for
 every $\bm Q\in\SO$, i.e.\ every rigid rotation superposed on the
 current configuration.
 \item[\textbf{(C3)}] \textbf{Symmetry}: the second Piola--Kirchhoff
 stress tensor satisfies $\bm S=\bm S^T$ (equivalently, in the Voigt
 notation~\eqref{eq:stress} used throughout, there is no independently
 parameterized $S_{21}\neq S_{12}$).
 \item[\textbf{(C4)}] \textbf{Normalization}: $\bm\sigma(\bm 0)=\bm0$ and
 $W(\bm0)=0$.
 \item[\textbf{(C5)}] \textbf{Polyconvexity}, in the sense of
 Ball~\cite{Ball1977}: see Section~\ref{sec:c5-definition} for its
 precise statement and Section~\ref{sec:c5-wrong} for its relation to a
 strictly stronger alternative condition used elsewhere in the
 literature.
 \item[\textbf{(C6)}] \textbf{Growth condition}: $W\to\infty$ as
 $\bm\varepsilon\to\infty$ (with extra terms if needed).
\end{enumerate}
Sections~\ref{sec:c1}--\ref{sec:c6} (within Section~\ref{sec:theory})
take each point in turn.  (C1)--(C4) are satisfied exactly, by
construction, independent of training.  (C6)'s qualitative coercivity
structure (a barrier as $J\to0^+$ and super-quadratic growth as any
structural stretch diverges) is likewise architectural rather than a
trained or fitted behavior, though, as Section~\ref{sec:c6} makes
precise, this stops short of the Sobolev-exponent matching a fully
rigorous existence proof would additionally require.
(C5) requires the most care: Section~\ref{sec:c5} gives Ball's precise
definition, the correct (weaker) necessary condition we actually
falsify-test, and the explicit construction that proves our model
polyconvex in Ball's sense; it also shows that a strictly stronger
alternative used elsewhere in the literature is not required by
polyconvexity and is, for this RVE's own ground-truth response, false.
Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction} places this checklist against the
current PANN literature as each piece of the construction is introduced:
which parts are already correctly and rigorously solved elsewhere, which
published claims are imprecise or documentedly incorrect, and which
combination of requirements (specifically, a \emph{general} anisotropic
material with no assumed symmetry group) remains an open problem that
this work targets.

\section{Admissibility of the construction: theoretical properties}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Kinematics and stress convention}
\label{sec:kinematics}
Let $\bm F$ be the deformation gradient, and
\begin{equation}
 \bm C=\bm F^T\bm F,\qquad \bm E=\tfrac12(\bm C-\bm I),\qquad J=\det \bm F=\sqrt{\det \bm C}>0.
 \label{eq:kinematics}
\end{equation}
The database and the model use the engineering-shear coordinate
\begin{equation}
 \bm\varepsilon=\begin{bmatrix}E_{11}\\E_{22}\\\gamma_{12}\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad \gamma_{12}=2E_{12},
 \label{eq:e-coordinate}
\end{equation}
so that the work identity
\begin{equation}
 dW=S_{11}\,dE_{11}+S_{22}\,dE_{22}+S_{12}\,d\gamma_{12},\qquad
 \bm\sigma:=\frac{\partial W}{\partial\bm\varepsilon}
 =\begin{bmatrix}S_{11}\\S_{22}\\S_{12}\end{bmatrix}
 \label{eq:stress}
\end{equation}
returns exactly the second Piola--Kirchhoff components stored in the
direct FOM data, with no factor-two ambiguity in the shear term and no
independently trained stress network: every stress reported in this
document is $\partial W/\partial\bm\varepsilon$ of the same scalar $W$.

\subsection{(C1) Thermodynamic consistency}
\label{sec:c1}
For a purely elastic RVE with no internal (dissipative) state variables,
the reduced isothermal Clausius--Duhem inequality is
$\mathcal D:=\bm\sigma^T\dot{\bm\varepsilon}-\dot W\geq0$.  Since
$W=W(\bm\varepsilon(t))$ and $W$ is differentiable,
\begin{equation}
 \dot W=\frac{\partial W}{\partial\bm\varepsilon}\cdot\dot{\bm\varepsilon}
 =\bm\sigma^T\dot{\bm\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
identically, for \emph{any} smooth scalar potential $W$ and \emph{any}
strain history $\bm\varepsilon(t)$, by the chain rule alone.  Hence
$\mathcal D\equiv0\geq0$: the inequality holds with equality, and the
model is thermodynamically consistent (fully reversible, zero
dissipation) for the same reason every hyperelastic (Green-elastic) model
is: because the stress is defined as $\partial W/\partial\bm\varepsilon$
in \eqref{eq:stress} and not as an independently parameterized function.
This is not a property that training can violate or that needs to be
checked numerically; it is a property of the functional form
\eqref{eq:stress} itself.

\subsubsection{A pure-regression baseline: violating (C1) directly}
\label{sec:c1-regression}
Section~\ref{sec:c1} shows (C1) holds for \emph{any} model of the form
$\bm S=\partial W/\partial\bm\varepsilon$, regardless of which $W$ is
learned. It says nothing about a model that is not of this form. As'ad et
al.~\cite{Asad2022} motivate their own hyperelastic-ANN construction by
first exhibiting exactly such a model, a plain regression network that
maps strain directly to stress with no potential anywhere, as their
baseline ``regression ANN'', the bottom tier of a three-tier ablation
(regression ANN, hyperelastic ANN, convex hyperelastic ANN) that
motivates each of their architectural commitments in turn. This section
repeats that ablation for this project's own RVE and its own admissibility
checklist, adding a fourth tier below the two certified polyconvex cores
and the uncertified free baseline of Section~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex}:

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Tier 1 (this section)}: a pure-regression MLP, widths
 $(\RegressionWidths{})$, mapping normalized $[E_{11},E_{22},\gamma_{12}]$
 straight to normalized $[S_{11},S_{22},S_{12}]$: the stress output is a
 direct forward pass, never a gradient of anything. No energy functional
 exists in this model at all.
 \item \textbf{Tier 2}: the free hyperelastic PANN of
 Section~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex}, an unconstrained energy $W_\theta$,
 so (C1) holds, but nothing else in (C4)--(C6) is guaranteed.
 \item \textbf{Tiers 3a/3b}: the certified polyconvex ICNN and ICKAN of
 Sections~\ref{sec:c5-construction} and~\ref{sec:ickan}, satisfying all
 six checklist items exactly.
\end{itemize}

We can test (C1) directly rather than just argue it. For a genuine hyperelastic
model, integrating $\dot W=\bm S\cdot\dot{\bm\varepsilon}$
(Section~\ref{sec:c1}) along any closed strain loop $\mathcal L$ (a path
in strain space with the same start and end point) gives
\begin{equation}
 \oint_{\mathcal L}\bm S\cdot d\bm\varepsilon
 =\oint_{\mathcal L}dW
 =W(\bm\varepsilon_{\rm end})-W(\bm\varepsilon_{\rm start})=0,
 \label{eq:cyclic}
\end{equation}
exactly, for \emph{every} closed loop, independently of which admissible
$W$ produced $\bm S$: this is a direct corollary of (C1), not a new
physical assumption. For the tier-1 pure-regression model, $\bm S$ is not the
gradient of anything, so nothing in its construction forces
\eqref{eq:cyclic}, and there is no reason to expect it numerically. We
evaluate \eqref{eq:cyclic} by trapezoidal quadrature ($\CyclicPointsPerEdge{}$
points per edge, justified below) on four closed strain loops entirely
inside the training range (Figure~\ref{fig:cyclic-loops}): a small
axis-aligned normal-strain square, a smooth curve that starts and
returns to the undeformed reference state $\bm\varepsilon=\bm0$ itself
while moving through all three strain components at once, a shear loop,
and an irregular triangle mixing normal and shear strain, for all four
tiers, using each model's own stress output (a direct forward pass for
tier 1, $\partial W_\theta/\partial\bm
\varepsilon$ by automatic differentiation for tiers 2--3b).

Accuracy does not reveal this violation; if anything, it hides it.
Table~\ref{tab:master} reports the tier-1 model's own test-trajectory accuracy: $\RegressionStressError{}$ overall stress error
and $\RegressionVonMisesError{}$ von Mises error, better than every

This is the sharpest version yet of the point made in
Section~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex} with the free baseline: an aggregate
accuracy metric on a test trajectory not only fails to detect the
absence of a physical constraint, it can reward the model that discards
the most physics, because a direct regression has no architectural
constraint competing with the stress fit at all. The cyclic-work test
of Table~\ref{tab:cyclic} measures something accuracy cannot: it is the
only test in this paper that checks (C1) itself, by direct observation
rather than as a metric to be optimized.

\subsection{(C2) Objectivity}
\label{sec:c2}
Objectivity concerns a superposed rigid rotation $\bm Q\in\SO$ of the
\emph{current} configuration, $\bm F\mapsto\bm Q\bm F$.  Since
\begin{equation}
 (\bm Q\bm F)^T(\bm Q\bm F)=\bm F^T\bm Q^T\bm Q\bm F=\bm F^T\bm F=\bm C,
\end{equation}
any scalar built only from $\bm C$ (equivalently, from $\bm F^T\bm F$, $\Cof(\bm F)^T\Cof(\bm F)=\Cof(\bm C)$,
and $\det \bm F$, as in Section~\ref{sec:c5}) automatically satisfies
$W(\bm Q\bm F)=W(\bm F)$ for every $\bm Q\in\SO$.  Every feature used by the model in
Section~\ref{sec:c5} is built exclusively from $\bm C$ and $\Cof(\bm C)$, so
objectivity holds identically, not approximately, and not because it was
included as a training penalty.  This is different from \emph{material}
symmetry, which concerns rotating the RVE relative to its own fixed
material axes and is discussed next.

\subsection{(C3) Symmetry: two different meanings}
\label{sec:c3}
The word ``symmetry'' is used for two unrelated properties in continuum
mechanics, and the checklist item (C3) refers to the first, not the
second:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Tensor symmetry}, $\bm\sigma=\bm\sigma^T$: a basic
 consequence of balance of angular momentum in the absence of body
 couples.  It must hold for \emph{any} admissible constitutive model,
 including anisotropic ones.
 \item \textbf{Material symmetry}: invariance of $W$ under a
 \emph{material} symmetry group (e.g.\ the $D_4$ group of a square
 lattice), $W(C_{11},C_{22},C_{12})=W(C_{22},C_{11},-C_{12})$.  This is a
 \emph{modelling choice}, not a conservation law, and this project
 deliberately does \emph{not} impose it: the RVE is treated as a general
 anisotropic material, because a square-shaped numerical cell does not by
 itself make the effective homogenized material isotropic or $D_4$
 symmetric.
\end{itemize}
Tensor symmetry (the actual content of C3) holds identically in this
model: the second Piola stress is parameterized by exactly the three
independent numbers $(S_{11},S_{22},S_{12})$ of \eqref{eq:stress}, which
are then placed into the symmetric matrix
$\begin{pmatrix}S_{11}&S_{12}\\S_{12}&S_{22}\end{pmatrix}$.
There is no separate, independently learned $S_{21}$ that could disagree
with $S_{12}$: $\bm\sigma=\bm\sigma^T$ by the structure of the
parameterization, not as an approximate or penalized identity. Material
symmetry ($D_4$) is a different, optional property that this model does
not claim.

\subsection{(C4) Normalization: the stress-free reference state}
\label{sec:c4}
\subsubsection{The energy value: trivial by construction}
Let $\Phi_\theta$ be a scalar network of a feature vector $\bm
z_{\rm pc}(\bm F)$ defined in Section~\ref{sec:c5}, and let $\bm
z_{\rm pc}(\bm I)$ denote the features evaluated at the reference state
$\bm F=\bm I$.  The energy is defined as
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm pc}(\bm F)=\Phi_\theta\big(\bm z_{\rm pc}(\bm F)\big)
 -\Phi_\theta\big(\bm z_{\rm pc}(\bm I)\big)-r\log J+\frac\beta2(J-1)^2 .
 \label{eq:pc-energy}
\end{equation}
At $\bm F=\bm I$: $\bm z_{\rm pc}(\bm F)=\bm z_{\rm pc}(\bm I)$ so the first two terms
cancel exactly; $J=1$ so $\log J=0$ and $(J-1)^2=0$.  Hence
$W_{\rm pc}(\bm I)=0$ \emph{exactly}, for \emph{any} network $\Phi_\theta$ and
\emph{any} parameters $\theta$, before any training happens.  This part
of (C4) requires no special argument about the structural directions.

\subsubsection{The stress value: not trivial, and this is where the balanced directions matter}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:pc-energy} and evaluating at $\bm\varepsilon=\bm0$
does \emph{not} automatically give zero: it requires the gradient of
the $\Phi_\theta$ term to be exactly cancelled by the gradient of the
$-r\log J$ term, and the latter only ever points in one fixed,
isotropic direction (equal normal parts, zero shear). The argument that
this cancellation actually happens has three steps: (i) show that at
the reference state, every one of the fifteen features feeding
$\Phi_\theta$ has a gradient that is a positive multiple of the
identity matrix $\bm I$: an \emph{isotropic} direction, regardless of how
anisotropic the feature itself is away from the reference state
(Lemma~\ref{lem:isotropic} and Proposition~\ref{prop:isotropic-features});
(ii) conclude, by the chain rule alone, that $\Phi_\theta$'s own
gradient at the reference state must then also be a non-negative
multiple of $\bm I$, however $\Phi_\theta$ was trained; (iii) show that a
single scalar $r$, matched to that multiple, exactly cancels it
against $-r\log J$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cancellation}). We take these
in turn.

\paragraph{Step (i): every feature's reference gradient is isotropic.}
The fact this step rests on is an elementary property of the
$2\times2$ cofactor applied to a rank-one, unit-norm outer product: it
turns the direction into its own orthogonal complement.

\begin{lemma}[Isotropic reference derivative]
\label{lem:isotropic}
Let $\bm d$ be a unit vector in $\R^2$.  Then $\Cof(\bm d\otimes\bm d)=\bm I-\bm d\otimes\bm d$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\bm d=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, so that
\begin{equation}
 \bm d\otimes\bm d=
 \begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\theta&\cos\theta\sin\theta\\[2pt]
 \cos\theta\sin\theta&\sin^2\theta\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The $2\times2$ cofactor formula (swap the diagonal entries, negate the
off-diagonal ones) gives
\begin{equation}
 \Cof(\bm d\otimes\bm d)=
 \begin{pmatrix}\sin^2\theta&-\cos\theta\sin\theta\\[2pt]
 -\cos\theta\sin\theta&\cos^2\theta\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
 -\begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\theta&\cos\theta\sin\theta\\[2pt]
 \cos\theta\sin\theta&\sin^2\theta\end{pmatrix}
 =\bm I-\bm d\otimes\bm d,
\end{equation}
using $\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$ on the diagonal entries.
\end{proof}

With this in hand, the same conclusion extends from a single direction
to every one of the fifteen features in~\eqref{eq:features}, provided
the directions within each system are balanced:

\begin{proposition}[Every feature has an isotropic $\bm C$-gradient at $\bm C=\bm I$]
\label{prop:isotropic-features}
Let $\bm d_{k1},\bm d_{k2},\bm d_{k3}$ be unit vectors and $w_{k1},w_{k2},w_{k3}>0$
satisfy the balance condition $\sum_iw_{ki}\,\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}=\bm I$ for
each system $k=1,2,3$, and recall the fifteen features of
Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction}: $I_1=\tr \bm C$, and, for $p=2,3$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{k,p}^F=\sum_iw_{ki}(\bm d_{ki}\cdot \bm C\bm d_{ki})^p,\qquad
 Q_{k,p}^H=\sum_iw_{ki}(\bm d_{ki}\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm d_{ki})^p .
\end{equation}
Then at $\bm C=\bm I$, every one of these thirteen scalars ($I_1$ and twelve
$Q$'s, three systems, two powers, two blocks), together with $J$ and
$J^2$, has a $\bm C$-gradient proportional to the identity: $dz_m/dC|_{\bm C=\bm I}
=c_m\,\bm I$ for every feature $z_m$, with the explicit coefficients $c_m$
collected in Table~\ref{tab:coefficients}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taking the trace of the balance identity and using $|\bm d_{ki}|=1$ gives
$\sum_iw_{ki}\,|\bm d_{ki}|^2=\tr(\bm I)=2$, i.e.\ $\sum_iw_{ki}=2$ for every
system $k$ (consistent with the numerical weights of
Table~\ref{tab:direction-systems}). This one identity, together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:isotropic}, is all four cases below need.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The invariant $I_1$.} Trivially, $dI_1/dC=\bm I$ for every
$\bm C$, so in particular at $\bm C=\bm I$.

\item \textbf{The $\bm F$-block features $Q_{k,p}^F$.} By the
product/chain rule,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dQ_{k,p}^F}{dC}=\sum_i p\,w_{ki}\,(\bm d_{ki}\cdot \bm C\bm d_{ki})^{p-1}\,\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}.
\end{equation}
At $\bm C=\bm I$, each factor $\bm d_{ki}\cdot \bm I\bm d_{ki}=|\bm d_{ki}|^2$
equals $1$, so the $(p-1)$-power drops out and only the balance sum
survives:
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{dQ_{k,p}^F}{dC}\right|_{\bm C=\bm I}=p\sum_iw_{ki}\,\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}=p\,\bm I.
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{The $\Cof(\bm C)$-block features $Q_{k,p}^H$.} This case
needs one extra observation before the same trick applies:
$\bm d\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm d$ is itself \emph{linear} in $\bm C$, because
$\bm d\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm d=\Cof(\bm C):(\bm d\otimes \bm d)$ and the cofactor
operation is self-adjoint under the Frobenius inner product for
$2\times2$ matrices ($A:\Cof(B)=B:\Cof(A)$, checked by direct
expansion), so $\bm d\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm d=\bm C:\Cof(\bm d\otimes\bm d)$: linear
in $\bm C$, with the \emph{constant} gradient
$\Cof(\bm d\otimes\bm d)=\bm I-\bm d\otimes \bm d$ supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:isotropic}. Differentiating as before,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dQ_{k,p}^H}{dC}=\sum_ip\,w_{ki}\big(\bm d_{ki}\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm d_{ki}\big)^{p-1}
 \big(\bm I-\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}\big).
\end{equation}
At $\bm C=\bm I$, $\Cof(\bm I)=\bm I$, so $\bm d_{ki}\cdot\Cof(\bm I)\bm d_{ki}=1$
and the $(p-1)$-power again drops out, leaving
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{dQ_{k,p}^H}{dC}\right|_{\bm C=\bm I}
 =p\Big[\Big(\textstyle\sum_iw_{ki}\Big)\bm I-\sum_iw_{ki}\,\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}\Big]
 =p\,(2\bm I-\bm I)=p\,\bm I,
\end{equation}
using $\sum_iw_{ki}=2$ and the balance identity a second time.

\item \textbf{The volumetric terms $J,J^2$.} The standard
determinant-derivative identity gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dJ}{dC}=\frac1{2J}\Cof(\bm C),\quad
 \left.\frac{dJ}{dC}\right|_{\bm C=\bm I}=\frac12\bm I;
 \qquad
 \frac{dJ^2}{dC}=2J\,\frac{dJ}{dC}=\Cof(\bm C),\quad
 \left.\frac{dJ^2}{dC}\right|_{\bm C=\bm I}=\bm I.
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\end{table}
In words: every feature's gradient at the reference state points in the
same isotropic direction $\bm I$; only the multiple $c_m$ changes from
feature to feature. This is exactly the fact Step (ii) needs.

\paragraph{Steps (ii)-(iii): the multiple cancels exactly against the volumetric barrier.}
By the chain rule, $\Phi_\theta$'s own reference gradient is a weighted
sum of the fifteen feature gradients just computed, each of which is a
multiple of $\bm I$, so the sum is too, automatically, for \emph{any}
trained $\Phi_\theta$. Matching that multiple against the barrier
term's own (also isotropic) reference gradient is what
Proposition~\ref{prop:cancellation} makes precise.

\begin{proposition}[Exact cancellation of the reference stress]
\label{prop:cancellation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 r:=\frac12\left.\left(\frac{\partial\Phi_\theta}{\partial E_{11}}+
 \frac{\partial\Phi_\theta}{\partial E_{22}}\right)\right|_{\bm\varepsilon=\bm0}.
 \label{eq:pressure-cancellation}
\end{equation}
Then $\bm\sigma(\bm0)=\bm0$ exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\Phi_\theta$'s reference gradient is $2\lambda \bm I$ for
some $\lambda\geq0$.} By the chain rule and
Proposition~\ref{prop:isotropic-features},
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{d\Phi_\theta}{dC}\right|_{\bm C=\bm I}
 =\sum_m\left.\frac{\partial\Phi_\theta}{\partial z_m}\right|_{\bm z(\bm I)}
 \left.\frac{dz_m}{dC}\right|_{\bm C=\bm I}
 =\Big(\sum_mc_m\left.\frac{\partial\Phi_\theta}{\partial z_m}\right|_{\bm z(\bm I)}\Big)\bm I
 =:\lambda \bm I .
 \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
Because $\Phi_\theta$ is an ICNN with non-negative weights at every
layer (Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction}), every partial derivative
$\partial\Phi_\theta/\partial z_m$ is non-negative; combined with
$c_m>0$ from Table~\ref{tab:coefficients}, $\lambda$ is a non-negative
combination of non-negative terms, so $\lambda\geq0$ automatically,
independent of training, for any $\theta$. Converting from $\bm C$ to $\bm E$
via $\bm C=\bm I+2\bm E$ doubles the gradient, $d\Phi_\theta/dE|_{\bm E=0}=2\lambda \bm I$,
which in the engineering-strain coordinate~\eqref{eq:e-coordinate} reads
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\partial\Phi_\theta}{\partial\bm\varepsilon}\right|_{\bm0}
 =(2\lambda,\,2\lambda,\,0)^T
\end{equation}
(the shear entry vanishes because it is the off-diagonal entry of the
isotropic matrix $2\lambda \bm I$).

\item \textbf{The barrier term's reference gradient has the same
isotropic shape.} Since $\log J=\tfrac12\log\det \bm C$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d(\log J)}{dC}=\frac{1}{2\det \bm C}\Cof(\bm C),
 \qquad
 \left.\frac{d(\log J)}{dC}\right|_{\bm C=\bm I}=\frac12\bm I;
\end{equation}
converting to $\bm E$ the same way as in Step~1 gives
$\partial(\log J)/\partial\bm\varepsilon|_{\bm0}=(1,1,0)^T$: the
same isotropic direction as Step~1's result, by construction.

\item \textbf{Match the two and cancel.} By
definition~\eqref{eq:pressure-cancellation} and Step~1,
$r=\tfrac12(2\lambda+2\lambda)=2\lambda$: $r$ is defined precisely so
that $r\,(1,1,0)^T$ equals $\Phi_\theta$'s own reference gradient
$(2\lambda,2\lambda,0)^T$ from Step~1. Summing all three terms
of~\eqref{eq:pc-energy} at $\bm\varepsilon=\bm0$ then gives
\begin{equation}
 \bm\sigma(\bm0)=\underbrace{(2\lambda,2\lambda,0)^T}_{\text{from }\Phi_\theta}
 \;-\;r\,\underbrace{(1,1,0)^T}_{\text{from }-r\log J}
 \;+\;\underbrace{\beta(J-1)\nabla J|_{J=1}}_{=\,0\text{, since }J=1}
 =\bm0. \qedhere
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The balance condition $\sum_iw_{ki}\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}=\bm I$ is what makes
this possible with a \emph{single} scalar $r$: without it, the reference
gradient of $\Phi_\theta$ would generally be an anisotropic $3$-vector
(unequal normal components, nonzero shear), and no scalar multiple of the
purely isotropic direction $(1,1,0)^T$ could cancel it exactly. The
construction would then leave a spurious residual stress at the
undeformed configuration: a real defect, unlike the SPD-tangent
distinction drawn in Section~\ref{sec:c5}, which does not indicate any
defect of the FOM at all.
\end{remark}

\subsection{(C5) Polyconvexity}
\label{sec:c5}

\subsubsection{Definition}
\label{sec:c5-definition}
Polyconvexity (Ball~\cite{Ball1977}) is a condition on the
\emph{functional form} of $W$ as a function of $\bm F$, namely that there
exists a jointly convex function $G$ of three \emph{independent} block
arguments such that
\begin{equation}
 W(\bm F)=G(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,\det \bm F),\qquad G\text{ convex on }\R^{2\times2}
 \times\R^{2\times2}\times\R.
 \label{eq:poly-def}
\end{equation}
This is an existence-theory condition: Ball used it (together with
suitable growth, Section~\ref{sec:c6}) to prove, via the direct method of
the calculus of variations, that minimizers of the total elastic energy
exist. It says nothing by itself about the sign of $\partial\bm\sigma/
\partial\bm\varepsilon$ at a given deformation state. The correct
hierarchy (Ball~\cite{Ball1977}; see also Dacorogna's standard reference
on the calculus of variations) is
\begin{equation}
 \text{convex}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{polyconvex}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{quasiconvex}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{rank-one convex},
 \label{eq:hierarchy}
\end{equation}
with each arrow strict in general; Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction}
proves our construction satisfies~\eqref{eq:poly-def} directly, and
Section~\ref{sec:c5-implies} gives the weakest, pointwise-checkable
necessary consequence of it that we verify numerically.

\subsubsection{A strictly stronger alternative used elsewhere, and why it is not adopted here}
\label{sec:c5-wrong}
Polyconvexity~\eqref{eq:poly-def} is not the only architectural route to
a well-posed hyperelastic model, and it is worth being precise about how
it relates to a different, strictly stronger condition used elsewhere in
the literature: a globally symmetric positive definite (SPD) tangent,
$\bm D=\partial\bm\sigma/\partial\bm\varepsilon\succ0$ everywhere.
\label{sec:related-asad}As'ad, Avery and Farhat~\cite{Asad2022} adopt
exactly this stronger condition for their own anisotropic fabric RVE,
built with the same FE$^2$ pipeline used here and validated inside an
actual coupled fluid--structure simulation (the supersonic inflation of a
parachute canopy, the only downstream robustness demonstration surveyed
in this paper), for a specific, well-documented reason, in their own
words: ``enforcing polyconvexity in $\bm F$ is difficult as $\bm E$ is
not in general convex in $\bm F$.'' Gao, Neff, Roventa and
Thiel~\cite{GaoNeffRoventaThiel2017} likewise build a valid
global-minimizer existence theorem directly on convexity of $W$ in $\bm C$,
which the same reparametrization $\bm C=\bm I+2\bm E$ shows is equivalent to this
SPD-tangent condition, and the same condition, enforced by an SPD-NN
predicting the tangent's Cholesky factor directly rather than an ICNN,
underlies Xu, Huang and Darve~\cite{Xu2021} for path-dependent
elasto-plastic anisotropic response. Both are legitimate, coherent design
choices, not errors, and As'ad et al.'s choice buys real robustness in
their finite-element examples (Newton's method diverges for a non-convex
ANN under load, converges for their convex-in-$\bm E$ one), but neither
is polyconvexity: Ball's existence theorem needs
only~\eqref{eq:poly-def} together with growth, not a globally SPD
tangent, so the two conditions are logically independent, and the
SPD-tangent route remains available only because, unlike plain convexity
of $W$ in $\bm F$ itself, convexity in $\bm C=\bm F^T\bm F$ raises no contradiction with
objectivity (objectivity forces $W$ to be constant along the curved,
non-convex orbit $\{\bm Q\bm F:\bm Q\in\SO\}$, which for $n=2$ includes $\bm Q=-\bm I$ and
would force convexity in $\bm F$ to control $W$ along a segment through the
singular matrix $\tfrac12(\bm F+(-\bm F))=0$; convexity in the already-objective
$\bm C$ has no such obstruction).

For this RVE specifically, however, the SPD-tangent route is not
available: the independent FOM tangent audit of this project measures
$\bm D=\partial\bm S/\partial\bm\varepsilon$ by finite differences at
several deformation states, including a test-trajectory state
where the \emph{smallest eigenvalue of $\bm D$ is $\FomStageTenTangentMinimum$},
i.e.\ negative. The true, homogenized RVE response is therefore not
SPD-tangent everywhere (a signature of an incremental-stability
transition in the underlying microstructure, not a modelling artifact),
and a surrogate forced to satisfy $\bm D\succ0$ globally would
necessarily \emph{contradict} the FOM at that state. We therefore target
the correct, weaker, and for this RVE actually achievable condition,
polyconvexity, and deliberately do not impose
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\bm S}{\partial\bm\varepsilon}\succ0 \qquad\text{(not imposed, and not implied by (C5))}.
 \label{eq:not-spd}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{The necessary condition we verify: rank-one convexity}
\label{sec:c5-implies}
The weakest property in the hierarchy~\eqref{eq:hierarchy}, rank-one
convexity, equivalent to the Legendre--Hadamard (ellipticity)
condition, is the correct \emph{necessary} consequence of
polyconvexity that can be checked pointwise:
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\right|_{t=0} W\big(\bm F+t\,\bm a\otimes\bm b\big)\;\geq\;0
 \qquad\text{for all }\bm F\text{ in the domain and all }\bm a,\bm b\in\R^2 .
 \label{eq:rank-one}
\end{equation}
This is a condition on the curvature of $W$ \emph{restricted to
rank-one lines in $\bm F$-space}, which is a small, highly structured subset
of directions, not on the full $3\times3$ Hessian $\bm D$ in strain
space required (and false) in Section~\ref{sec:c5-wrong}. Rank-one
convexity failing is the classical mathematical signature of loss of
ellipticity (shear banding, microstructure buckling): a real, physically
meaningful phenomenon that a good model should be able to reproduce, not
one it should be forced to exclude. \eqref{eq:rank-one} is what the
evaluator in this project samples: $64$ random pairs $(\bm F,\bm a,\bm b)$ with $\det
\bm F>0$, giving a smallest observed value of $\RankOneCurvature$. Being
positive is consistent with (necessary for) polyconvexity; it is a
numerical falsification attempt, not the proof, which is given next.

\subsubsection{The construction, and the proof that it is polyconvex}
\label{sec:c5-construction}
For each $k=1,2,3$, fix three unit structural directions $\bm d_{ki}$ and
positive weights $w_{ki}$ satisfying the balance condition
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i=1}^3w_{ki}\,\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}=\bm I,
 \label{eq:balanced-directions}
\end{equation}
with the three direction systems (angles in degrees) and weights recorded
in Table~\ref{tab:direction-systems}, chosen without reference to any
material symmetry group: Section~\ref{sec:direction-selection} gives the
explicit, reproducible procedure that produced them. A symmetric,
direction-built structural tensor entering the energy alongside the
classical isotropic invariants is a classical device, not one introduced
here: it traces to Boehler~\cite{Boehler1987} and
Spencer~\cite{Spencer1971,Spencer1984}. A discrete set of weighted
directions satisfying an exactness condition analogous
to~\eqref{eq:balanced-directions}, there for scalar polynomial quadrature
on the sphere rather than for this tensor identity, is independently used
by Ba\v{z}ant and Oh~\cite{BazantOh1986} for numerical integration.

\label{sec:related-klein-linden}This is deliberately not tied to any
named symmetry group, which is worth placing against how the two closest
prior polyconvex constructions handle the same requirement. Klein et
al.~\cite{Klein2022} (the primary reference for the correct polyconvexity
definition used throughout Section~\ref{sec:c5}) give two ICNN
constructions restricted a priori to a symmetry group $\mathcal G\subset
SO(3)$ (cubic, transversely isotropic) via a structural tensor: their
invariant-based model $W^I$ gets objectivity, material symmetry, and
polyconvexity exactly, but not the reference state, and they say why
directly: an exact stress-free correction ``preserves polyconvexity when
formulated in terms of $\bm F$ instead of $\bm C$, [but] is not
compatible with the methods of incorporating objectivity and material
symmetry which are used in this work''; their deformation-gradient model
$W^F$ trades the opposite way, exact reference state and polyconvexity,
but objectivity only approximated by data augmentation over $1024$
random rotations. Linden et al.~\cite{Linden2023} close this specific gap
for isotropic and transversely isotropic materials, achieving all four
properties \emph{simultaneously} within a fixed structural-tensor
symmetry class, but, as already noted in
Section~\ref{sec:introduction}, do not extend further. Klein et
al.~\cite{Klein2026} widen the named-group set considerably (cubic,
tetragonal, \dots) via a triclinic integrity basis of $\bm C,\Cof(\bm
C),J$ restricted by an explicit group-symmetrization operator, though
every worked example (their Section~5) still uses the cubic group
specifically, and raise, without developing it into a proof or example,
that the same model ``could also be employed without incorporating
material symmetry at all'' (Appendix~\ref{app:klein-trivial} examines
what their own machinery would already imply in this limit, and why we
do not credit it as an achieved result). This is the open problem this
paper answers for a $2$D RVE with no assumed symmetry
class:\label{sec:related-gap} the balanced, non-$D_4$-closed direction
systems of Table~\ref{tab:direction-systems} replace the structural
tensor and are not tied to any symmetry group, while
Proposition~\ref{prop:isotropic-features} still supplies the isotropic
reference-derivative structure the correction needs, giving, from here
onward, an explicit statement of the general-material case and a proof
against (C1)--(C6) not yet carried out anywhere in this literature,
complementing rather than competing with Klein et al.'s
differently-scoped result for named symmetry groups.

\paragraph{How the direction systems are chosen: an explicit,
reproducible procedure.}
\label{sec:direction-selection}
Fixing three angles $\theta_{k1},\theta_{k2},\theta_{k3}\in[0^\circ,180^\circ)$
for a candidate system $k$ determines the unit directions
$\bm d_{ki}=(\cos\theta_{ki},\sin\theta_{ki})$; the balance
condition~\eqref{eq:balanced-directions} is then three scalar equations
for the three unknown weights $w_{k1},w_{k2},w_{k3}$, obtained by
equating each of the three independent entries of the symmetric
$2\times2$ matrix $\sum_iw_{ki}\bm d_{ki}\otimes \bm d_{ki}$ to the
corresponding entry of $\bm I$:
\begin{equation}
 \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}
 \cos^2\theta_{k1}&\cos^2\theta_{k2}&\cos^2\theta_{k3}\\
 \sin^2\theta_{k1}&\sin^2\theta_{k2}&\sin^2\theta_{k3}\\
 \sin\theta_{k1}\cos\theta_{k1}&\sin\theta_{k2}\cos\theta_{k2}&\sin\theta_{k3}\cos\theta_{k3}
 \end{pmatrix}}_{=:A(\theta_{k1},\theta_{k2},\theta_{k3})}
 \begin{pmatrix}w_{k1}\\w_{k2}\\w_{k3}\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:weight-system}
\end{equation}
For a generic angle triple, $A$ is invertible and
\eqref{eq:weight-system} has a unique solution $\bm w_k$ from a single
$3\times3$ linear solve, but that solution is not guaranteed to be
admissible: the construction additionally needs $w_{ki}>0$ (used
explicitly in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:polyconvex}, where
positivity of the weights is what lets a sum of convex terms stay
convex), and nothing in \eqref{eq:weight-system} forbids a negative or
zero entry for a poorly chosen triple. The systems of
Table~\ref{tab:direction-systems} were produced by the direct,
mechanical procedure this makes possible: sample a candidate angle
triple, solve \eqref{eq:weight-system} for $\bm w_k$, and keep the
triple only if $A$ is well-conditioned and every solved weight is
strictly positive; repeat until three sufficiently distinct systems are
found. The three reported systems are well-conditioned in this sense
($\mathrm{cond}(A)\approx1.9$, $2.3$, $2.2$ for systems $1$--$3$
respectively) and their solved weights agree with
Table~\ref{tab:direction-systems} to the reported precision.

Two honest limits of this procedure are stated plainly here rather than
left implicit. First, none of the proofs in this section use any
property of these specific nine angles beyond
\eqref{eq:balanced-directions} and $w_{ki}>0$: Propositions~\ref{prop:isotropic-features}
and~\ref{prop:polyconvex} hold verbatim for \emph{any} three systems
produced by the same accept/reject procedure, so the particular numbers
in Table~\ref{tab:direction-systems} are a convenient, verified choice,
not a load-bearing one. Second, and more fundamentally, this paper does
\emph{not} prove that the resulting map from $\bm C$ to the
$15$-dimensional feature vector~\eqref{eq:features} is injective (that
two different admissible $\bm C$ can never produce identical features),
nor that three direction systems are the minimum or optimal number for
this construction: a single system already supplies an admissible,
polyconvex model, and more than three would remain admissible by the
identical argument. Three systems were adopted because, empirically,
they gave the anisotropic expressivity needed to fit this RVE's own
test-trajectory response (Section~\ref{sec:results}) with a single fixed
feature set, not because of a representational-completeness argument
for a general anisotropic material. This is a reasonable, structured
construction consistent with every proof and every numerical result in
this paper, not a theorem of representational optimality
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations} returns to this point).

From these we form
\begin{align}
 I_1&=\tr \bm C=|\bm F|^2,\nonumber\\
 Q_{k,p}^F&=\sum_{i=1}^3 w_{ki}\,(\bm d_{ki}\cdot \bm C\bm d_{ki})^p
       =\sum_{i=1}^3 w_{ki}|\bm F\bm d_{ki}|^{2p},\qquad p=2,3,\nonumber\\
 Q_{k,p}^H&=\sum_{i=1}^3 w_{ki}\,(\bm d_{ki}\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm d_{ki})^p
       =\sum_{i=1}^3 w_{ki}|\Cof(\bm F)\bm d_{ki}|^{2p},\qquad p=2,3,
 \label{eq:qfeatures}
\end{align}
using $\bm C:\Cof(A\otimes A)$ identities as in Lemma~\ref{lem:isotropic} to
recognize $\bm d\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm d=|\Cof(\bm F)\bm d|^2$. The evaluated feature vector is
\begin{equation}
 \bm z_{\rm pc}=(I_1,\{Q_{k,2}^F,Q_{k,3}^F,Q_{k,2}^H,Q_{k,3}^H\}_{k=1}^3,J,J^2)
 \in\R^{15}.
 \label{eq:features}
\end{equation}
For a single direction and $p=1$, $Q_{k,1}^F$ reduces to the classical
pseudo-invariant $\bm a\cdot\bm C\bm a$ used throughout anisotropic
hyperelasticity~\cite{Spencer1971,Spencer1984,Boehler1987}; Schr\"oder and
Neff~\cite{SchroderNeff2003} prove polyconvexity of exactly this
invariant, and of its $\Cof(\bm C)$-based counterpart, raised to any power
for a single fixed direction, which Proposition~\ref{prop:polyconvex}
below extends to balanced sums over multiple directions. Gasser, Ogden
and Holzapfel~\cite{Gasser2006} generalize the single-direction invariant
to a continuous integral over a fiber-orientation density, the continuous
analogue of the discrete weighted sum used here.

The sixth-power terms are not cosmetic. In two dimensions a balanced
quartic aggregate alone obeys an accidental $90^\circ$ identity: writing
$t_i=\bm d_i\cdot \bm C\bm d_i$, the $90^\circ$-rotated directions have $t_i'=\tr(\bm C)-t_i$
(true for \emph{any} unit vector and its orthogonal complement, by trace
decomposition in an orthonormal basis), and the balance identity gives
$\sum_iw_i(\tr(\bm C)-t_i)^2=\sum_iw_it_i^2$ exactly (expand the square and
use $\sum_iw_it_i=\tr(\bm C)$ and $\sum_iw_i=2$). The cubic aggregate
$\sum_iw_it_i^3$ does not obey this identity in general, so it supplies
genuine anisotropic information while (Proposition~\ref{prop:isotropic-features})
its first derivative at $\bm C=\bm I$ remains isotropic.

\begin{remark}[The fifteen features are not algebraically independent]
\label{rem:redundancy}
The $Q^H$ features are, in fact, exact algebraic functions of $I_1$ and
the $Q^F$ features, so the fifteen entries of $\bm z_{\rm pc}$
in~\eqref{eq:features} are not fifteen independent scalars. This does
not weaken the construction (Propositions~\ref{prop:isotropic-features}
and~\ref{prop:polyconvex} hold regardless), but the two cases are not
equally justified. $Q_{k,3}^H$ earns its own network input for a
concrete architectural reason: its exact reconstruction from
$I_1,Q_{k,2}^F,Q_{k,3}^F$ requires negative coefficients that a
monotone (non-negative-weight) ICNN cannot represent internally, so
supplying it directly gives the network access to combinations it
could not otherwise reach. $Q_{k,2}^H$ has no such justification: the
identity $Q_{k,2}^H=Q_{k,2}^F$ (an exact 2D consequence of
$\Cof\bm C=\tr(\bm C)\bm I-\bm C$, verified to machine precision for
the trained model) has coefficient $+1$, which a monotone network
can already represent by weighting $Q_{k,2}^F$ alone; $Q_{k,2}^H$
therefore adds no expressiveness in this specific 2D construction. We
retain it anyway, for uniformity with the general $F$/$H$ recipe
(built from $\Cof\bm F$ exactly as $Q^F$ is built from $\bm F$, for
every $k$ and every $p$), which is dimension-independent and does not
exhibit this collapse in 3D, where $\Cof\bm C$ is a genuinely
quadratic (not affine) function of $\bm C$. Appendix~\ref{app:redundancy}
gives the exact identities and this reasoning in full.
\end{remark}

Let $\Phi_\theta:\R^{15}\to\R$ be an input-convex neural network (ICNN,
Amos et al.~\cite{Amos2017}) with Softplus activations and \emph{every}
weight matrix (input-to-hidden at every layer, hidden-to-hidden, and
hidden-to-output) reparameterized as $\mathrm{softplus}(\cdot)+
\varepsilon_0>0$. Non-negativity at every layer (not just hidden-to-hidden,
as in the original ICNN convexity-only construction) is what additionally
makes $\Phi_\theta$ \emph{coordinate-wise non-decreasing} in $\bm
z_{\rm pc}$, a property used explicitly below. The energy is
\eqref{eq:pc-energy}, repeated here with $r,\beta>0$:
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm pc}(\bm F)=\Phi_\theta(\bm z_{\rm pc})-\Phi_\theta(\bm z_{\rm pc}(\bm I))
 -r\log J+\frac\beta2(J-1)^2.
 \tag{\ref*{eq:pc-energy}}
\end{equation}

Figure~\ref{fig:icnn-diagram} collects this whole construction end to
end: the deformation gradient, the 15-dimensional feature map, the
input-convex architecture itself (widths $15$-$64$-$64$-$32$-$1$,
Softplus activations, every weight non-negative, with the
characteristic ICNN skip connections feeding $\bm z_{\rm pc}$ directly
into every layer rather than only the first), the resulting polyconvex
energy, and the stress obtained from it by automatic differentiation.

The literature documents two concrete, checkable errors of exactly this
kind, useful context for how easy this is to get wrong even with a
stated intent to be rigorous. Klein et al.~\cite{Klein2022} report that
Ghaderi et al.~\cite{Ghaderi2020} claim a polyconvex ANN potential while
using hyperbolic-tangent activations, which are not convex; we
cross-checked this against the original, whose own Section~4 confirms
the network core uses ``soft plus $\dots$, sinusoid $\dots$ and
hyperbolic tangent'' activations together, none of which is convex and
non-decreasing throughout. They report the same failure mode for Tac et
al.\ (2021), whose model uses the non-polyconvex invariant $\bar
I_2=\tfrac12(\tr\bar C)^2-\tfrac12\tr\bar C^2$ as a direct input
(Hartmann and Neff, cited by Klein et al.\ as their Lemma~2.4). Both
illustrate the same point this construction is built to avoid:
polyconvexity is a property of the specific functional form all the way
down to the activation function and the input invariants, not a label
attachable to an architecture in general.

\begin{proposition}[Polyconvexity of $W_{\rm pc}$]
\label{prop:polyconvex}
$W_{\rm pc}(\bm F)=G(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,J)$ for a function $G$ that is jointly convex
on $\R^{2\times2}\times\R^{2\times2}\times(0,\infty)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The argument has four stages: each of the fifteen features is convex in
its own block variable; a function of only one block of a convex,
block-separated feature is therefore convex in the joint variable
$(\bm F,\Cof\bm F,J)$; $\Phi_\theta$ composed with this feature map stays
convex by the vector composition rule; and the remaining terms of
$W_{\rm pc}$ are themselves convex or constant.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Each feature is convex in its own block.} For a fixed unit
vector $\bm d$, $\bm F\mapsto|\bm F\bm d|^2$ is a convex quadratic form in
$\bm F$ (a composition of the linear map $\bm F\mapsto \bm F\bm d$ with the
convex norm-squared). For $p\in\{2,3\}$, $x\mapsto x^p$ is convex and
non-decreasing on $x\geq0$, and $|\bm F\bm d|^2\geq0$; by the scalar
convex-composition rule ($g$ convex non-decreasing, $f$ convex
$\Rightarrow g\circ f$ convex), $|\bm F\bm d|^{2p}$ is convex in $\bm F$, and
summing with positive weights preserves convexity, so every $Q_{k,p}^F$
is convex in $\bm F$. The identical argument with $\bm F$ replaced by
$\Cof \bm F$ shows every $Q_{k,p}^H$ is convex in $\Cof \bm F$. Finally,
$I_1=|\bm F|^2$ is convex in $\bm F$, and $J,J^2$ are (trivially, being
affine and quadratic) convex functions of the third block variable.

\item \textbf{Hence convex in the joint variable.} Each of these $15$
functions, viewed as a function of the joint variable
$(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,J)\in\R^{2\times2}\times\R^{2\times2}\times\R$, depends
on only one block and is convex in that block, hence is convex in the
joint variable (a function of a linear projection of a convex function
is convex).

\item \textbf{$\Phi_\theta$ composed with the feature map stays
convex.} $\Phi_\theta$ is convex in $\bm z_{\rm pc}$ and non-decreasing
in each coordinate (weight non-negativity at every layer, standard ICNN
induction: each Softplus pre-activation is an affine,
non-negative-weighted combination of convex non-decreasing quantities of
the input, hence convex and non-decreasing, and Softplus itself is
convex and non-decreasing, so the composition is too, by induction on
layers). By the \emph{vector} convex-composition rule (Boyd \&
Vandenberghe, \S3.2.4: $h$ convex and non-decreasing in each argument,
each $g_i$ convex $\Rightarrow$ $h(g_1,\dots,g_n)$ convex),
$\Phi_\theta(\bm z_{\rm pc}(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,J))$ is convex in
$(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,J)$.

\item \textbf{The remaining terms, and the sum.} The additive constant
$-\Phi_\theta(\bm z_{\rm pc}(\bm I))$ does not affect convexity, and
$-r\log J$ and $\tfrac\beta2(J-1)^2$ are convex functions of $J>0$ for
$r,\beta>0$. A sum of functions each convex in $(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,J)$ is
convex in $(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,J)$; setting $G$ equal to this sum proves the
proposition.
\end{enumerate}
Note that this establishes convexity for $(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,J)$ ranging over
the whole product space, i.e.\ genuinely as three \emph{independent}
block arguments, which is exactly \eqref{eq:poly-def}, not merely along
the constrained manifold $\{(\bm F,\Cof \bm F,\det \bm F):\bm F\in\GLp\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is a construction proof: polyconvexity is a property of the
functional form \eqref{eq:pc-energy}--\eqref{eq:features}, holding for
\emph{every} value of $\theta$, not a property enforced by a training
penalty that could be violated between checkpoints or degraded by future
retraining with a different loss (Section~\ref{sec:loss}). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:polyconvex} and \eqref{eq:hierarchy}, $W_{\rm pc}$
is automatically also rank-one convex, and this is what the numerical
sample in Section~\ref{sec:c5-implies} attempts to falsify (and fails
to).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Why $r\geq0$ automatically]
By \eqref{eq:lambda}, $r=2\lambda$ with $\lambda$ a non-negative
combination (positive coefficients $c_m$, non-negative $\partial_{z_m}
\Phi_\theta$) of non-negative terms, so $r\geq0$ for \emph{any} trained
$\theta$, not only the reported one.
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection{A second convex core: input-convex Kolmogorov--Arnold network (ICKAN)}
\label{sec:ickan-construction}
Every proposition above is stated for ``any $\Phi_\theta$ that is convex
and coordinate-wise non-decreasing in $\bm z_{\rm pc}$'': none of them
use any property specific to the Softplus ICNN core. This subsection
replaces $\Phi_\theta$ with an input-convex Kolmogorov--Arnold network
(ICKAN), following Thakolkaran et al.~\cite{Thakolkaran2025} and using
their reference implementation
(\url{https://github.com/mmc-group/ICKANs}), while keeping every other
part of the construction (the balanced direction systems, the
$15$-dimensional feature vector $\bm z_{\rm pc}$, the reference-state
cancellation of Proposition~\ref{prop:cancellation}, and the volumetric
barrier of Section~\ref{sec:c6}) identical to
Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction}.

A Kolmogorov--Arnold network replaces each scalar weight of a standard
layer with a learned univariate spline. In the reference implementation,
every such spline is reparameterized so that its control points $c_i$
satisfy $c_{i+1}-c_i\geq0$ and $c_{i+2}-2c_{i+1}+c_i\geq0$ (a double
cumulative sum of non-negative increments): the first condition makes
the spline non-decreasing, the second makes it convex, for \emph{any}
value of the underlying trainable parameters. Disabling the
(non-convex) residual base function and freezing the inter-layer affine
maps to the identity (exactly the configuration used by Thakolkaran
et al.\ and reused here) means every edge of the network is
simultaneously convex and non-decreasing in its scalar input, and a sum
of such edges is again convex and non-decreasing by the same rule used
for the ICNN. We verified this numerically before training: for a
randomly initialized ICKAN core, increasing one input strictly
increases the output, and the output is convex along an arbitrary line
through feature space. Consequently,
Propositions~\ref{prop:cancellation}--\ref{prop:polyconvex} apply to
this model without modification, confirmed directly by measurement in
Section~\ref{sec:ickan}, where the reference stress norm for the
trained ICKAN model matches the ICNN model's at machine precision.

\subsection{(C6) Growth condition}
\label{sec:c6}
Ball's existence theorem pairs polyconvexity with a coercivity/growth
condition on $G$, schematically
\begin{equation}
 G(\bm F,H,\delta)\geq c_1|\bm F|^p+c_2|H|^q+h(\delta),\qquad p,q\geq2,
\end{equation}
with $h$ convex, $h(\delta)\to\infty$ as $\delta\to0^+$ and superlinearly
as $\delta\to\infty$, needed so that minimizing sequences of the total
energy do not escape to infinity and a minimizer exists. Our construction
supplies this structure explicitly:
\begin{itemize}
\item The sixth-power terms $Q_{k,3}^F,Q_{k,3}^H\sim|\bm F\bm d|^6,|\Cof(\bm F)d|^6$
give super-quadratic polynomial growth in $\bm F$ and $\Cof \bm F$ as any
structural stretch diverges; Proposition~\ref{prop:sixth-power-bound}
below makes this precise with an explicit, direction-independent lower
bound, rather than a qualitative appeal to direction coverage.
\item The volumetric term $h(J)=-r\log J+\tfrac\beta2(J-1)^2$ diverges as
$J\to0^+$ (impenetrability of matter: infinite energy to crush the RVE to
zero volume), exactly the qualitative shape used in classical polyconvex
volumetric penalties (e.g.\ Ciarlet--Geymonat-type terms). As $J\to\infty$,
$-r\log J$ alone \emph{decreases} without bound; it is the quadratic
term that guarantees $h(J)\to\infty$ eventually, for any $\beta>0$,
since a quadratic dominates a logarithm asymptotically regardless of
the constants involved. With the coefficients learned by this model
($r=\BarrierCoefficient$, $\beta=\QuadraticCoefficient$), that crossover
occurs only at $J^\ast\approx9550$ (the root of $h'(J)=0$ for $J>1$), an
area dilation never approached by this RVE's training or test data;
Figure~\ref{fig:volumetric-isolated} isolates $h(J)$ and confirms this
directly.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[A direction-independent lower bound on the sixth-power terms]
\label{prop:sixth-power-bound}
For any single balanced system ($\sum_iw_i\,\bm d_i\otimes \bm d_i=\bm I$, $w_i>0$)
and any $\bm F$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{k,3}^F=\sum_{i=1}^3w_i\,(\bm d_i\cdot \bm C\bm d_i)^3\;\geq\;\tfrac14\,|\bm F|^6,
 \qquad
 Q_{k,3}^H\;\geq\;\tfrac14\,|\bm F|^6,
 \label{eq:sixth-power-bound}
\end{equation}
where $|\bm F|^2=\tr \bm C$ is the Frobenius norm. In particular
$Q_{k,3}^F,Q_{k,3}^H\to\infty$ as $|\bm F|\to\infty$ along \emph{every}
direction, not merely the three structural ones: with no appeal
needed to how many orientations the balanced system happens to cover.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $t_i=\bm d_i\cdot \bm C\bm d_i\geq0$. Two consequences of the balance
condition, both already used in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:isotropic-features}, are all this needs:
$\sum_iw_i=2$, and, contracting the balance identity with $\bm C$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_iw_i\,\bm d_i\cdot \bm C\bm d_i=\bm C:\sum_iw_i\bm d_i\otimes \bm d_i=\bm C:\bm I=\tr \bm C,
 \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
 \sum_iw_it_i=|\bm F|^2.
\end{equation}
Since $x\mapsto x^3$ is convex on $x\geq0$, Jensen's
inequality applied with weights $w_i/2$ (which sum to $1$) gives
\begin{equation}
 \Big(\sum_i\frac{w_i}2\,t_i\Big)^{\!3}\;\leq\;\sum_i\frac{w_i}2\,t_i^3,
 \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
 \Big(\frac{|\bm F|^2}2\Big)^{\!3}\leq\frac12\,Q_{k,3}^F,
\end{equation}
which rearranges to $Q_{k,3}^F\geq\tfrac14|\bm F|^6$. The identical
argument with $\bm C$ replaced by $\Cof(\bm C)$ gives $Q_{k,3}^H\geq\tfrac14
|\Cof \bm F|^6$; in two dimensions $|\Cof \bm F|=|\bm F|$ exactly (the cofactor of
a $2\times2$ matrix permutes and sign-flips entries without changing
the sum of their squares), so $Q_{k,3}^H\geq\tfrac14|\bm F|^6$ as well.
\end{proof}

We do not claim to have verified the precise Sobolev-exponent matching
that a fully rigorous existence proof for the associated boundary-value
problem would require; that is a mathematical refinement beyond the scope
of this paper. What Proposition~\ref{prop:sixth-power-bound} and the
volumetric barrier above establish, together, is that the
\emph{qualitative} coercivity structure Ball-type theorems require is
present by construction and provably direction-independent, not added
as an afterthought penalty or asserted by appeal to intuition.

Placing this against the third cluster of Section~\ref{sec:introduction},
which gets polyconvexity right but rarely exercises (C6) in
practice:\label{sec:related-cann} Linka \& Kuhl~\cite{Linka2023}, Tac et
al.~\cite{Tac2023}, and Thakolkaran et al.~\cite{Thakolkaran2025} all
correctly use the additive, block-decoupled sufficient condition for
polyconvexity (convex, non-decreasing scalar functions of $I_1$, of a
polyconvex modification of $I_2$, and of $J$, summed), and Tac et al.\
are unusually careful to distinguish this from the strictly weaker
rank-one convexity; but the growth condition (C6), while stated by all
three, is uniformly \emph{not exercised}, since Linka \& Kuhl and Tac et
al.\ both work with (nearly) incompressible materials, so $J\to0^+$ is
never actually reached by a trained model, and Thakolkaran et al.'s sole
volumetric term, $K_3=(J-1)^2$, stays finite at $J=0$. Our own volumetric
term is exercised, not merely stated (Table~\ref{tab:coefficients}--\ref{tab:direction-systems}
and Figures~\ref{fig:volumetric-isolated}--\ref{fig:volumetric} sample
$W_{\rm pc}$ down to $J=10^{-6}$ for a compressible, general anisotropic
material), and Section~\ref{sec:ickan-construction} shows the same
construction transfers, proof-for-proof, to Thakolkaran et al.'s own
ICKAN architecture: no other work surveyed here combines an ICKAN core
with a general, group-free anisotropic feature set. Two further
isotropic-only works round out this cluster: Tac, Sahli-Costabal and
Buganza Tepole~\cite{Tac2022} originate the neural-ODE route to the same
monotone-invariant sufficient condition on anisotropic (two-fixed-fiber)
skin data, again without an exercised $J\to0^+$ barrier, while
Abdolazizi, Aydin, Cyron and Linka~\cite{Abdolazizi2025} apply an
unconstrained Kolmogorov--Arnold network to the same isotropic invariants
and decline any convexity requirement at all, with good fits and no
reported instability, consistent with (not evidence against) the point
Section~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex} makes directly on our own data: losing
the polyconvexity certificate does not necessarily show up as a visible
failure on in-distribution data.

\section{Projection-based reduced-order model}
\label{sec:rom-comparison}
This section fixes notation for the PROM used in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} as an independent, non-ANN-constitutive-law
comparison point for the PANN of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. None of the
constructions in this section are a contribution of this work; they are
established tools, cited to their sources, applied here to the RVE and
FOM of Section~\ref{sec:background-fom}. The section builds a single
online pipeline in three stages, not three competing constructions: a
base, linear projection-based reduced basis (below); corrected by a
trained nonlinear-manifold decoder, PROM-ANN
(Section~\ref{sec:background-decoder}); evaluated with a hyper-reduced
cubature rule, HPROM-ANN (Section~\ref{sec:background-ecm}). HPROM--ANN
and D-HPROM--ANN, evaluated separately in Section~\ref{sec:results}, are
in turn two \emph{operating modes} of that same hyper-reduced pipeline
and the same trained decoder, not two different models: HPROM--ANN runs
the decoder's prediction through the usual Newton correction
of~\eqref{eq:prom-residual} to convergence, while D-HPROM--ANN simply
skips that correction and uses the decoder's own prediction directly, on
the premise that it is already a sufficiently good estimate of the
reduced state on its own. Section~\ref{sec:background-decoder}
introduces the decoder once; the split into these two modes is stated
precisely where it arises, at the end of that subsection.

A standard linear PROM replaces $\bm u_f$ by its projection onto a low-dimensional
subspace. Snapshots $\bm u_f(\bm\varepsilon_1),\dots,\bm u_f(\bm\varepsilon_{n_{\rm s}})$ from FOM
solves along the training trajectories
(Fig.~\ref{fig:training-trajectories}) are assembled into a snapshot matrix
$\bm D\in\mathbb R^{n_L\times n_{\rm s}}$, and a thin, truncated singular
value decomposition gives an orthonormal reduced-order basis (ROB)
$\mathbf V_{\rm tot}\in\mathbb R^{n_L\times n_{\rm tot}}$ spanning the
dominant left singular subspace of $\bm D$. The displacement field is then
approximated by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prom-affine}
 \bm u_f\approx \bm u_f^{\rm aff}(\bm\varepsilon)+\mathbf V_{\rm tot}\,\bm q_{\rm tot}(\bm\varepsilon),
\end{equation}
where $\bm u_f^{\rm aff}(\bm\varepsilon)$ is the affine baseline consistent with the
prescribed macroscopic Dirichlet data $\bm u_d(\bm\varepsilon)$ (so that
$\mathbf V_{\rm tot}\bm
q_{\rm tot}$ carries only the correction beyond that affine guess) and
$\bm q_{\rm tot}\in\mathbb R^{n_{\rm tot}}$ are the generalized (POD)
coordinates.

Substituting~\eqref{eq:prom-affine} into~\eqref{eq:fom-residual}
and testing with the same basis (Galerkin projection, the natural choice
here since the tangent operator of a hyperelastic problem is symmetric,
unlike the non-self-adjoint operators that motivate a least-squares
Petrov--Galerkin, LSPG, test space in the compressible-flow PROM
literature~\cite{Barnett2023,AresDeParga2026}) gives the reduced equilibrium
equation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prom-residual}
 \mathbf V_{\rm tot}^T\,\bm r\big(\bm u_f^{\rm aff}(\bm\varepsilon)+\mathbf V_{\rm tot}\bm q_{\rm tot};\bm\varepsilon\big)=\bm 0,
\end{equation}
a system of $n_{\rm tot}\ll n_L$ nonlinear equations, again solved by
Newton--Raphson. As documented for this exact class of problem
(propagating features, evolving nonlinear regions) by
Barnett and Farhat~\cite{Barnett2022} and by Hern\'andez's own metamaterial and
damage examples~\cite{HernandezMAWECM}, a single linear subspace
of dimension small enough to be worth reducing to is generally \emph{not}
enough to reach high accuracy for a nonlinear problem (the Kolmogorov
$n$-width barrier): the number of retained modes needed for good accuracy
can remain uncomfortably large. The nonlinear-manifold construction of
this paper, PROM-ANN, addresses exactly this point.

\subsection{Nonlinear-manifold PROM: PROM-ANN}
\label{sec:background-decoder}
Following the general nonlinear-PMOR strategy of decomposing the ROB into a
small \emph{primary} (retained) part and a larger \emph{secondary}
(discarded) part whose amplitude is reconstructed by a trained nonlinear map,
introduced for fluid-dynamics PMOR as the PROM-ANN/HPROM-ANN framework
by Barnett, Farhat and Maday~\cite{Barnett2023} and generalized to
interpretable Gaussian-process and radial-basis-function closures by Ares De
Parga et al.~\cite{AresDeParga2026}. PROM-ANN denotes this paper's own
instance of that framework: the same base PROM above, still using every
Gauss point of the original mesh, but with the discarded modes'
amplitude reconstructed by a trained decoder instead of being dropped
outright. The total basis is partitioned as
$\mathbf V_{\rm tot}=[\mathbf V\ \bar{\mathbf V}]$, $\mathbf V\in\mathbb
R^{n_L\times n}$, $\bar{\mathbf V}\in\mathbb R^{n_L\times\bar n}$,
$n\ll\bar n$, and the displacement field is approximated as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decoder}
 \bm u_f\approx \bm u_f^{\rm aff}(\bm\varepsilon)+\mathbf V\,\bm q+\bar{\mathbf V}\,\mathcal N(\bm q),
\end{equation}
with $\bm q\in\mathbb R^n$ the primary (retained) generalized coordinates
and $\mathcal N:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^{\bar n}$ a trained neural network
that reconstructs the secondary (discarded) amplitudes ($n=3$, $\bar
n=36$ for the trained model used throughout this paper).

It is natural to ask why the primary coordinates are not simply the
first three raw POD coordinates. The raw POD coordinates $\mathbf
Q_{\rm tot}=\mathbf V_{\rm tot}^T\mathbf S$
of a snapshot matrix $\mathbf S$ are optimal, by construction, for
linear reconstruction accuracy: no other rank-$n$ subspace captures more
snapshot energy. They are not, however, optimized for what the decoder
in~\eqref{eq:decoder} actually needs downstream: a coordinate that a
regression map $\bm\varepsilon\mapsto\bm q$ can learn accurately.
Figure~\ref{fig:pod-coordinates} shows why this matters concretely for
this RVE: the first three raw POD coordinates of the training
trajectories (Fig.~\ref{fig:training-trajectories}) trace out a
curved, self-intersecting ribbon in $\mathbb R^3$ (left panel) that
bears little resemblance to the simple, boustrophedon sweep of the
macro-strain domain itself (right panel): a nonlinear, non-monotonic
relationship that is unnecessarily hard for a regression network to
learn well. We therefore do not use $\mathbf Q_{\rm tot}$ directly;
instead, $\mathbf V$ and $\bm q$ are identified via the same
least-squares primary/secondary basis-identification procedure used by
Hern\'andez's MAW--ECM paper~\cite{HernandezMAWECM} for a different
homogenization RVE: a linear transformation of the raw POD coordinates,
fitted so that the resulting \emph{structured} coordinates track the
macro-strain path as closely as possible in a least-squares sense,
while remaining an exact orthonormal change of basis within the
primary subspace (so~\eqref{eq:decoder}'s reconstruction accuracy is
unaffected). The middle panel of Figure~\ref{fig:pod-coordinates} shows
the effect on this RVE's own data: the same ten training trajectories,
expressed in the structured coordinates, recover the simple,
near-affine sweep pattern of the macro-strain domain itself, at a
verified affine-fit accuracy of $6.9\times10^{-4}$ relative
$L^2$ error ($R^2\geq0.99999$ componentwise) and a
primary/secondary orthogonality residual of
$2.3\times10^{-15}$ (machine precision). This is exactly
what makes the direct macro-strain-to-coordinate regression
in~\eqref{eq:decoder} tractable: $\mathcal N$ only has to learn the
much smaller residual nonlinearity between this already near-affine
structured coordinate and the discarded secondary amplitudes, not the
raw POD manifold's own curvature.

The decoder network $\mathcal N$ itself (Figure~\ref{fig:decoder-network})

In this project, $\mathbf V$ and $\bm q$ are identified with the
macro-strain $\bm\varepsilon$ itself, an \emph{input-driven} manifold, valid here
because the problem is static and hyperelastic (no history dependence), so
the equilibrium state is uniquely parameterized by $\bm\varepsilon$. Consequently
$\mathcal N$ is trained once, offline, on the training trajectories to map
macro-strain directly to the reconstructed secondary coordinates, and no
reduced equilibrium equation of the form~\eqref{eq:prom-residual} needs to
be solved online at all if $\mathcal N$'s prediction is trusted directly;
alternatively, \eqref{eq:decoder} can still be substituted into a
Galerkin-projected residual and corrected by Newton iterations, exactly as
in~\eqref{eq:prom-residual}. These are precisely the two HPROM--ANN
operating modes evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:results}: the
\emph{iterative} mode (Newton correction to convergence) and the
\emph{direct} mode, D-HPROM--ANN (zero Newton iterations, $\mathcal N$'s
output used as-is).

\subsection{Hyperreduction: HPROM-ANN via the manifold-adaptive-weight empirical cubature method (MAW--ECM)}
\label{sec:background-ecm}
HPROM-ANN is PROM-ANN above with one further reduction: replacing the
full Gauss-point loop itself by a sparse, learned cubature rule. Even
after~\eqref{eq:decoder}, evaluating the residual~\eqref{eq:fom-residual}
(or the reduced residual~\eqref{eq:prom-residual}) still requires a full
loop over all $n_{\rm gp}=\sum_e n_{\rm gp}^{(e)}$ Gauss points of the
original FOM mesh, since the stress $\bm S_{e,g}$ must be evaluated from
the true microscopic constitutive law at every one of them. Reindexing the
element/Gauss-point pairs $(e,g)$ of~\eqref{eq:fom-residual} by a single
global index $g\in\{1,\dots,n_{\rm gp}\}$ (as is standard in the ECM
literature), so that $\bm r_g:=\bm L_e^T\bm B_{e,g}^T\bm S_{e,g}$ and
$\bm S_g:=\bm S_{e,g}$ denote the residual and stress contribution of
global Gauss point $g$, hyperreduction replaces the full Gauss-point sum
by a sparse cubature rule over a small subset $\mathcal
Z\subset\{1,\dots,n_{\rm gp}\}$ of retained points with positive weights
$\{\omega_g\}_{g\in\mathcal Z}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ecm-residual}
 \sum_{g\in\mathcal Z}\big(\mathbf V^T\bm r_g\big)(\bm u_f;\bm\varepsilon)\,\omega_g=\bm 0,
 \qquad
 \bm S_{\rm macro}(\bm\varepsilon)\approx\frac{1}{|\Omega_0|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal
 Z_\sigma}\bm S_g(\bm u_f;\bm\varepsilon)\,\omega_{\sigma,g},
\end{equation}
one rule ($\mathcal Z$, weights $\omega_g$) tailored to integrate the
projected \emph{equilibrium} residual accurately, and, in general, a second,
independently constructed rule ($\mathcal Z_\sigma$, weights
$\omega_{\sigma,g}$) tailored to the homogenized-\emph{stress} output
functional: the two point sets and weight sets are not required to
coincide, and Fig.~\ref{fig:ecm-supports} shows both retained element sets
for this RVE side by side.

The base weights, for a single fixed macro-strain state, are found
offline by the ECM of Hern\'andez, Caicedo
and Ferrer~\cite{Hernandez2017}, using the greedy active-set
sparse non-negative least-squares algorithm of Hern\'andez's own later
formulation~\cite{Hernandez2020}: starting from an empty point set, the
element whose residual-basis column best explains the current
integration-error vector is added at each step, the weights on the
active set are recomputed by a constrained least-squares solve, and any
element whose weight has gone negative is purged and re-solved, until
the truncated-SVD basis of the training-snapshot integrand space is
integrated to within a prescribed tolerance. This is in the same spirit
as (and predates) the energy-conserving-sampling-and-weighting (ECSW)
family used for Petrov--Galerkin hyperreduction in the fluids PMOR
literature~\cite{Farhat2014,Farhat2015,Grimberg2021} and the empirical
quadrature procedure~\cite{An2008,PateraYano2017,Yano2019}. A model
that uses a single such \emph{fixed} weight set for every macro-strain
state is an ECM-hyperreduced HPROM.

The manifold-adaptive-weight idea behind MAW--ECM, introduced by
Hern\'andez~\cite{HernandezMAWECM}, instead lets $\omega_g$ (and
$\omega_{\sigma,g}$) vary smoothly with the reduced state, here the
macro-strain, so that the same support set $\mathcal Z$ stays accurate
over a wider region of the strain path than a fixed rule could.
Hern\'andez's own construction achieves this smoothness through a
graph-Laplacian-regularized pruning-and-regression scheme over a
discretized manifold of candidate states, and the construction deployed
for this RVE follows the same two-stage idea, in the same spirit,
though not a line-for-line reproduction of the published implementation.

\emph{Stage one: graph-regularized support pruning.} Starting from a
larger candidate support already produced by the fixed-weight ECM
procedure above (one candidate weight vector per training state), a
two-phase backward-elimination procedure prunes this candidate set down
to the final, fixed support $\mathcal Z$ (or $\mathcal Z_\sigma$)
reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:ecm-supports}. In phase one, candidates are
removed one at a time, independently at each training state, whenever
the remaining active set can still satisfy that state's linear
moment-matching constraint by a least-squares reweighting with no
weight going negative, until an intermediate support size of $40$
points is reached. In phase two, elimination continues jointly across
states: a $k$-nearest-neighbor graph ($k=8$) is built over a uniform
subsample of $500$ training states (over the reduced coordinate $\bm
q$ for the equilibrium-residual rule, or over the macro-strain
$\bm\varepsilon$ itself for the two homogenized-stress rules), and, at
each step, every remaining low-activity candidate is trial-removed by
solving a constrained quadratic program that keeps every sampled
state's weight vector close to its previous value while penalizing the
graph-Laplacian roughness of the weight field across neighboring states
(smoothing weight $\alpha=10^2$); the removal that leaves the smoothest
resulting weight field is kept, subject to the same non-negativity and
moment-matching constraints as phase one, until the final target size
of $10$ points is reached. This graph-regularized second phase is what
actually supplies the manifold-adaptive smoothness Hern\'andez's
construction is designed to give: it is a genuine, non-trivial
regularization step acting jointly across training states, not a
relabeling of independent per-state ECM solves.

\emph{Stage two: continuous weight-field regression.} Phase two's
output is the final fixed support together with a table of
graph-smoothed weight vectors, one per sampled training state: still
a discrete object that must be evaluated at arbitrary, unseen states
online. This table is fit by one of two regressors depending on the
rule: a Gaussian radial-basis-function model with a polynomial trend
for the equilibrium-residual rule, and a softmax-output neural network
for the two homogenized-stress rules (non-negativity and the fixed row
sum $\sum_g\omega_g$ guaranteed by the softmax itself), trained with a
loss combining a Kullback--Leibler term and a mean-squared-error term
against the pruned weight table, plus a physics-exactness penalty that
enforces the linear integration constraint over the \emph{full}
training set, not only the $500$-state subsample used for pruning. This
follows Hern\'andez's manifold-adaptive-weight idea in spirit rather
than reproducing the published algorithm line for line. The offline
scripts that produced the specific support and weights deployed for
this RVE are not part of this project's own contribution; the trained
weights themselves are, and are exactly what every HPROM--ANN number in
this paper is computed from.

More broadly, related work on reduced-order modeling
for computational
homogenization~\cite{Hernandez2014,Hernandez2020,Miehe1999} traces back
at least to the POD-based reduced model multiscale method (R3M) for

work on hyperreduction for Petrov--Galerkin ROMs and parallel/digital-twin

and on machine-learning-assisted homogenization of masonry by two of

\subsection{Loss design: an equivalent-stress-oriented training choice}
\label{sec:loss}
Sections~\ref{sec:c1}--\ref{sec:c6} are properties of the energy
functional \eqref{eq:pc-energy} and hold regardless of how the model is
trained. The training loss is a different kind of choice: a purely
statistical one, made because it measurably improves accuracy on the
quantity of engineering interest, and it does not touch
\eqref{eq:pc-energy} at all. It is worth describing carefully, because
none of the polyconvex-PANN constructions of Table~\ref{tab:literature}
discuss a risk we found here: per-component loss normalization, used in
its most extreme form by As'ad et al.~\cite{Asad2022}, can measurably
hurt accuracy on the physically relevant equivalent stress when the
material's stress components differ by orders of magnitude.

Every model reported in this paper is trained on the plain two-term sum
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\EnergyWeight\,\mathcal L_W+\mathcal L_{S,\rm agg},
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_W$ normalizes the mean squared energy error by its mean
squared target, and $\mathcal L_{S,\rm agg}$ normalizes the mean squared
error of the full stress vector $(S_{11},S_{22},S_{12})$ by the mean
squared norm of the full target vector, so it is dominated by whichever
components carry the most physical stress magnitude. In both terms the
predicted stress is $\partial W_{\rm pc}/\partial\bm\varepsilon$ of the
predicted energy, as in \eqref{eq:stress}.

We arrived at \eqref{eq:loss} by rejecting an alternative that looked
reasonable at first. In this RVE, $S_{12}$ is roughly two orders of
magnitude smaller than $S_{11}$, $S_{22}$ along both the training
trajectories and the test trajectory alike, which makes it
tempting to give it its own dedicated weight. The previously certified
version of this model did exactly that, blending in a second,
componentwise stress term,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_S(\gamma)=(1-\gamma)\,\mathcal L_{S,\rm agg}+\gamma\,\mathcal L_{S,\rm comp},
 \qquad \gamma\in[0,1],
 \label{eq:loss-blend}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{S,\rm comp}$ is the equally weighted mean of the
three componentwise relative-squared errors (each of $S_{11}$,
$S_{22}$, $S_{12}$ normalized by its own mean squared target before
averaging, rather than by the norm of the full vector). At
$\gamma=\ComponentStressWeightOld{}$, the value used previously, the
optimizer is forced to reduce $S_{12}$'s relative error to the same
standard as $S_{11}$, $S_{22}$, even though $S_{12}$ contributes
negligibly to the plane-stress equivalent (von Mises) stress
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{\rm eq}=\sqrt{S_{11}^2-S_{11}S_{22}+S_{22}^2+3S_{12}^2},
 \label{eq:von-mises}
\end{equation}
the quantity of actual engineering interest here. As'ad et
al.~\cite{Asad2022} push exactly this kind of per-component weighting to
its most extreme form.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates this choice from the other change made
at the same time, widening the ICNN. Widening alone, with
$\gamma=\ComponentStressWeightOld{}$ unchanged, gives a small
improvement over the original network; setting $\gamma=0$ instead,
dropping $\mathcal L_{S,\rm comp}$ entirely, gives essentially all of
the remaining gain, and, somewhat counter-intuitively, improves
$S_{12}$'s own componentwise error rather than trading it away for a
better fit elsewhere. The likely explanation is that the componentwise
term was forcing the optimizer to chase a noisy, two-orders-of-magnitude-smaller
relative target at the expense of the rest of the fit, rather than any
genuine tension between the components. Since $\gamma=0$ is what
actually works best, \eqref{eq:loss-blend} appears here only as the
rejected alternative that motivates \eqref{eq:loss}, and plays no
further role in this paper.

\subsection{Training protocol}

The model uses all $109\,460$ saved states of the ten training

\subsection{Test-trajectory assessment: all six model variants}
\label{sec:results}
Table~\ref{tab:master} reports relative $L^2$ errors against the direct
FOM labels, including the plane-stress equivalent (von Mises) stress
\eqref{eq:von-mises}, for all six model variants considered in this
paper: the four ANNs of Section~\ref{sec:c1-regression} (tiers
1--3b), and the two hyper-reduced projection-based ROMs used elsewhere in
this project as an independent, non-ANN-constitutive-law point of
comparison on the identical test path. HPROM--ANN and D-HPROM--ANN
are two operating modes of the same hyper-reduced solver and the same
trained solution-manifold ANN (mapping macro-strain directly to reduced
POD coordinates via the primary/secondary decoder of
Section~\ref{sec:background-decoder}, unrelated to the constitutive-law
ANNs of tiers 1--3b), rather than two different models: HPROM--ANN runs
the full ECM-weighted Newton corrector to convergence at every step (up
to $25$ iterations), while D-HPROM--ANN sets the corrector iteration
count to zero and simply evaluates the manifold ANN's direct
macro-strain-to-reduced-state prediction, with no equilibrium solve at
all. Both reconstruct stress from the true microscale Neo-Hookean law at
ECM-selected Gauss points rather than from a learned constitutive
surrogate, so they measure a different source of error, hyper-reduction
and manifold-regression error, than tiers 1--3b's constitutive-surrogate
error. (This construction is not itself a contribution of this paper;
Section~\ref{sec:rom-comparison} fixes its notation, cites its sources
in full, and places it against the broader reduced-order-modeling
literature.)

There is a further complication worth spelling out. HPROM--ANN's native
output is a naive volume-average of the converged microscopic
strain/stress field, not the exactly energy-conjugate macro-stress that
tiers 1--3b are trained and evaluated against (the same
regression-vs.-conjugate distinction of Section~\ref{sec:c1-regression},
one level up the pipeline). For a mixed Dirichlet/free boundary-value
problem these two macroscopic stress definitions need not agree, and
empirically they do not: a direct check found the two differing by
roughly $30\%$ on this RVE. Comparing HPROM--ANN's raw output against the
other five rows below would therefore not be comparing like with like,
so instead we compute the same energy-conjugate quantity for
HPROM--ANN's own converged state. By the envelope theorem at an
equilibrium point, this reduces to a reaction-force-weighted sum over
the Dirichlet-constrained boundary nodes, with no re-solving or finite
differences needed. We verified that this reaction-force procedure
reproduces the training data's own energy-conjugate stress to a
relative $L^2$ error of $\HpromControlFomStressError$, effectively
machine precision, on a held-out subset of the training trajectories,
before applying it to the test-trajectory HPROM--ANN/D-HPROM--ANN
states reported below.

\subsection{A second convex core: ICKAN results}
\label{sec:ickan}
Section~\ref{sec:ickan-construction} shows that the same construction of
Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction}, the balanced direction systems, the
$15$-dimensional feature vector, the reference-state cancellation, and
the volumetric barrier, carries over unchanged to the ICKAN core
following Thakolkaran et al.~\cite{Thakolkaran2025}, and explains why
the same propositions apply to it without modification
(Section~\ref{sec:related-cann} places this relative to the ICKAN
literature). Measurement confirms it directly: the reference stress
norm for the trained ICKAN model is $\IckanReferenceStressNorm$, at
machine precision, exactly as for the ICNN model.

The ICKAN core used here has hidden widths $(\IckanHiddenWidths{})$, a
grid of $\IckanGrid{}$ intervals, and spline order $\IckanSplineOrder{}$.
It is trained with the same loss \eqref{eq:loss} as the ICNN model,
reaching its best tracked training score at epoch $\IckanBestEpoch{}$;
the ICNN and ICKAN rows of Table~\ref{tab:master} and
Figure~\ref{fig:full-comparison} report its test-trajectory
accuracy alongside the ICNN model. The smallest sampled rank-one
curvature is $\IckanRankOneCurvature$, and the broad sampled-energy
audit gives a minimum of $\IckanSampledMinimumEnergy$: both positive, as
for the ICNN model, consistent with the polyconvexity certificate
without amounting to a proof of it.

The two cores turn out to be close, with neither uniformly better. The
ICKAN reaches comparable accuracy with a hidden-layer parameter count
roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than the ICNN's
$(\IcnnWidths{})$, though each epoch costs more because of the spline
evaluations. Both certificates are architectural and independent of
which core is used, so choosing between them in practice comes down to
parameter budget and training cost rather than admissibility.

\subsection{What breaks without the certificate: a non-polyconvex baseline}
\label{sec:non-polyconvex}
As'ad et al.~\cite{Asad2022} and Klein et al.~\cite{Klein2022} each
include a flexible, uncertified baseline in their own work for exactly
this reason (Section~\ref{sec:related-asad}): to show what the
certificate is protecting against. As'ad et al.\ show Newton's method
diverging under load; Klein et al.\ show their flexible model losing
ellipticity even for small deformations (their Fig.~5). We repeat that
exercise on this project's own RVE, using the flexible ``free''
anisotropic PANN already introduced for the original, three-model
version of this study, an unconstrained Softplus MLP on the material
components of $\bm C$ with no convexity constraint anywhere in the
architecture, retrained with the identical loss \eqref{eq:loss} used
for the two certified models above so that the comparison isolates the
convexity architecture as the only difference.

Trained this way, the free model reaches $\FreeMatchedEnergyError$
energy error and $\FreeMatchedVonMisesError{}$ von Mises error on the
test trajectory, comparable to, and on this metric marginally
better than, the certified polyconvex models of Table~\ref{tab:master}
(its $S_{12}$ error, $\FreeMatchedSxyError$, is much worse, consistent
with Section~\ref{sec:loss}). Figure~\ref{fig:full-comparison} shows its
von Mises trajectory is visually indistinguishable from the certified
models and from the direct FOM. Accuracy alone, in other words, gives no
hint that a polyconvexity certificate is missing. Abdolazizi et al.\
made the same observation about their uncertified
CKAN~\cite{Abdolazizi2025} on the basis of their own reported results
(Section~\ref{sec:related-cann}); here it is demonstrated directly on
our own model and our own data.

The absence of the certificate is nonetheless real and findable, once
the right test is applied. We sampled $\FreeRankOneSamples$ random
rank-one perturbations $\bm F+t\,\bm a\otimes\bm b$ (unit $\bm a,\bm b$,
log-stretch range $\pm1$, i.e.\ stretches in $[e^{-1},e^{1}]$) and
evaluated $d^2/dt^2\,W$ at $t=0$ in float64, the same falsification
attempt of \eqref{eq:rank-one} run on the certified models in
Section~\ref{sec:c5-implies}, at a larger sample count. Every sample was
non-negative for the certified ICNN and ICKAN models (Table~\ref{tab:master}'s
discussion and Section~\ref{sec:results}); for the free model,
$\FreeRankOneViolations$ of $\FreeRankOneSamples$ samples
($\FreeRankOneViolationFraction$) are negative, with a minimum of
$\FreeRankOneMinimumCurvature$, comparable in magnitude to the
certified models' minimum \emph{positive} sampled curvature. One
example violation is
\begin{equation}
 \bm F=\begin{bmatrix}0.534&-0.372\\-0.387&0.728\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \bm a=\begin{bmatrix}0.939\\-0.345\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \bm b=\begin{bmatrix}-0.064\\-0.998\end{bmatrix},\quad
 \frac{d^2W}{dt^2}\bigg|_{t=0}<0,
 \label{eq:free-violation}
\end{equation}
a moderate, unremarkable compressive-shear state ($\det \bm F\approx0.24$),
not an extreme or contrived one. This is exactly what
Section~\ref{sec:c5-implies} argues: rank-one convexity is the correct
necessary consequence of polyconvexity, and without an architectural
guarantee it can fail at ordinary deformation states while every accuracy
metric looks fine. The certified models in this paper do not merely
happen to avoid this on the states sampled; Proposition~\ref{prop:polyconvex}
proves they cannot fail it anywhere, by construction.

\subsection{A genuine FE\texorpdfstring{$^2$}{\textsuperscript{2}} demonstration: Cook's membrane}
\label{sec:cook}
Every result so far certifies the energy functional itself, or evaluates
it at the level of the RVE's own test trajectory. As'ad et
al.~\cite{Asad2022} and Klein et al.~\cite{Klein2022} go one step
further and show that an uncertified model can make Newton's method
fail inside an actual structural solve; this section does that too, but
also goes a step further still. At every macroscopic Gauss point of a
structural solve, the macroscopic Newton loop needs a homogenized stress
and tangent from ``the microscale.'' This section supplies that need
three distinct ways, all deployed on the identical macroscopic problem so
they can be compared directly against one another, and against the true
multiscale answer, rather than only against each other's own test trajectory: (i) each of the four PANN tiers (regression, tier 1; free
hyperelastic, tier 2; polyconvex ICNN, tier 3a; polyconvex ICKAN, tier
3b), evaluated directly -- a fully-amortized, trained surrogate for the
\emph{entire} microscale solve, effectively free online, since every bit
of microscale physics it uses was compressed into its trained weights
offline; (ii) D-HPROM--ANN and HPROM--ANN (Section~\ref{sec:rom-comparison}),
nested as the actual RVE-level solver at every Gauss point -- a genuine,
if reduced-order, online microscale solve, not a stand-in, denoted
D-HPROM--ANN-FE$^2$ and HPROM--ANN-FE$^2$ below to distinguish this
nested deployment from the same trained models' test-trajectory
evaluation of Table~\ref{tab:master}; and (iii) the
true, non-reduced $990$-element RVE FOM itself, nested the same way, with
no reduction of any kind, denoted FOM-FE$^2$ -- the actual multiscale
ground truth this whole hierarchy approximates. To our knowledge this is the first time a
physics-augmented constitutive surrogate, a reduced-order projection-based
surrogate, and a genuinely nested full-order microscale solve have all
been compared directly, on the identical macroscopic problem, rather than
only against each other on a test trajectory.

The problem is Cook's membrane: a tapered panel with corners
$(0,0),(48,44),(48,60),(0,44)$ (SI units), clamped on its left edge and
loaded by a ramped, in-plane shear force on its right edge, a standard
large-deformation benchmark in the nonlinear finite-element literature,
not tied to any single surveyed paper. It has no hole and no periodic
structure, and its dominant loading is tensile, matching, rather than
fighting, this project's own training range
(Section~\ref{sec:data-campaign}): the compressive and shear excursions
this panel actually develops stay inside, or close to, the training
window, so the comparison below is not a test of extrapolation. The
final load ($2.08\times10^8\,\mathrm N$, ramped linearly over $20$
steps) was chosen for exactly this reason: it is the largest load, on
this mesh, for which the certified models' own strain state
($E_{11}\in[-0.039,0.012]$, $\gamma_{12}\in[-0.032,0.059]$) stays inside
the trained range of Section~\ref{sec:data-campaign}, while still
producing genuinely large deformation (a tip displacement of
$3.1$--$3.3\,\mathrm m$ on this $48$--$60\,\mathrm m$ panel, depending on
which of the seven rows of Table~\ref{tab:cook-fe2} below is asked).

\paragraph{Setup.} The panel is meshed, generated directly for this
geometry rather than imported, with $128$ six-node quadratic triangles
($384$ Gauss points, $n_x=n_y=8$), coarser than this project's usual
mesh density, for tractability: even at this resolution, nesting the
true, non-reduced RVE FOM at every Gauss point (the FOM-FE$^2$ row of
Table~\ref{tab:cook-fe2}) costs on the order of a day of wall-clock
time (see below). All seven rows use the identical mesh, load ramp, and
Total-Lagrangian Newton-Raphson driver, built on this project's own
Kratos Multiphysics~\cite{Dadvand2010} assembler
(Section~\ref{sec:background-fom}), differing only in which function
supplies the stress and tangent at each Gauss point: each trained PANN's
own automatic-differentiation stress and tangent (Section~\ref{sec:results},
verified against finite differences before use: relative tangent error
$1.1\times10^{-10}$ for the ICNN and ICKAN cores, $1.4\times10^{-4}$ for
the float32 free and regression models) for the first four rows; the
consistent, non-finite-difference analytic tangent derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:consistent-tangent} for D-HPROM--ANN and HPROM--ANN;
and a central finite-difference tangent, the only option available, for
the true FOM itself. Every load step uses the same convergence tolerance
(relative residual $10^{-4}$) and the same secant line search used
throughout this project's solvers, except for the true-FOM row, run
without line search (plain Newton already converges it, and line search
would triple that row's own already-considerable cost).

ROM/HPROM surrogates, and the true FOM itself converge at every one of the
$20$ load steps, in exactly three Newton iterations each, with no change in
iteration count from the smallest to the largest load. The two uncertified
tiers both fail to converge already at the \emph{first} load step, only

\paragraph{Small-strain accuracy and a corrective loss term.} The
certified cores' accuracy is not uniform across the strain range. At the
small strains Cook's own first load step visits ($|\bm\varepsilon|$ from
$6\times10^{-7}$ to $3\times10^{-3}$), both carry an $8$--$15\%$ stress
error against the true FOM, an order of magnitude worse than their
test-trajectory error (Table~\ref{tab:master}); the two ROM/HPROM
surrogates and the uncertified tier-2 free model all stay within $3\%$
there. We attribute this to two compounding factors. The
loss~\eqref{eq:loss} normalizes the stress term by the aggregate
mean-squared target \emph{over the whole training set}
(Section~\ref{sec:loss}), so small-stress, small-strain states carry
almost no training signal; and, since the free model does not share
this weakness despite an identical loss, the input-convexity constraint
itself costs some representational flexibility near the reference
state, a real price of the certificate distinct from the aggregate
accuracy cost already reported as negligible.

Only the loss-weighting factor is directly correctable, without
touching the polyconvexity construction, the features, or the training
data. We add one further term,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm tan0}=\frac{\big\|\mathbb C_\theta(\bm 0)-\mathbb
 C_0\big\|^2}{\|\mathbb C_0\|^2},\qquad
 \mathcal L=\EnergyWeight\,\mathcal L_W+\mathcal L_{S,\rm agg}+w_{\rm tan0}\,\mathcal L_{\rm tan0},
 \label{eq:loss-tan0}
\end{equation}
matching the model's own tangent at zero strain, $\mathbb C_\theta(\bm
0)$, against the RVE's true linear-elastic-limit tangent $\mathbb C_0$ --
the one reference value in this paper needing no nonlinear FOM solve at
all, since three linear RVE solves, one per unit macro-strain direction,
give it exactly. Retraining the certified ICNN and ICKAN cores with
$w_{\rm tan0}=5$ (found by a small sweep, Appendix~\ref{app:smallstrain-sweep},
not fine-tuned further) drops their first-load-step error to
$0.2$--$1.3\%$ and $0.9$--$2.8\%$ respectively; this is the version
Table~\ref{tab:cook-fe2} reports throughout. The correction has a real
cost elsewhere: both models' error on the full test trajectory of
Table~\ref{tab:master} rises from sub-percent to the low single digits,
since the new term competes for the same limited model capacity. Since
that metric is not sensitive to the small-strain regime this term
targets, Table~\ref{tab:master} keeps the original, $w_{\rm tan0}=0$
cores; Table~\ref{tab:cook-fe2-corrected} quantifies the trade directly.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limitations}
Three scope limits of the present study are worth stating plainly, since
each bears directly on how far the claims above extend.

The construction of Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction} assumes no
material symmetry group, and the balanced direction systems are not tied
to one; this is a property of the construction itself. The specific RVE
used to validate it, however,
a square unit cell with a centered circular hole
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rve-geometry}), does have $D_4$ symmetry, so the test-trajectory accuracy of Section~\ref{sec:results} does not by itself demonstrate the
construction's behavior on a genuinely asymmetric microstructure.
Repeating the same protocol on an RVE with no symmetry at all, an
off-center or elliptical hole, or a random inclusion pattern, would
close this gap directly.

A second gap is more theoretical: as noted in
Section~\ref{sec:direction-selection}, this paper does not prove that
two distinct admissible $\bm C$ always produce distinct feature
vectors~\eqref{eq:features}, nor that three direction systems are the
minimum or optimal number. The construction is verified sufficient for
this RVE's own test-trajectory response, not shown to be representationally
optimal or complete for a general anisotropic material.

Finally, some ablations and cost figures are missing. Sensitivity to
the number of direction systems (one or two instead of three), to the
specific angles within a system, and to the random seed used for
training are not reported here, nor is the online evaluation cost of
the certified PANN relative to a direct microscale solve or to
HPROM--ANN, which would be needed to argue for the certified
construction on efficiency grounds rather than admissibility grounds
alone. The test-trajectory comparison of Section~\ref{sec:results} also uses a
single test trajectory; a broader set of test paths, including
states well outside the convex hull of the training trajectories, would
give a fuller picture of extrapolation behavior.

None of this weakens the results already reported: every admissibility
proof in Sections~\ref{sec:c1}--\ref{sec:c6} holds unconditionally,
independent of which RVE, which direction systems, or which downstream
solver is used. These are open extensions, and we would rather name
them here than leave a reader to find them.

Section~\ref{sec:cook} already takes the further step of nesting
D-HPROM--ANN, HPROM--ANN, and the true, non-reduced RVE FOM itself as the
RVE-level solver inside the macroscopic Newton loop of its own benchmark,
at every macroscopic Gauss point, rather than deploying any surrogate
directly, at one fixed mesh ($n_x=8$, $384$ Gauss points) shared by all
seven rows of Table~\ref{tab:cook-fe2}. We do not report a
mesh-convergence study of this benchmark, and make no claim that its
macroscopic answer is itself resolution-independent: that question is
orthogonal to the one the comparison is built to ask. Its purpose is to
rank fidelity tiers -- fully-amortized surrogate, reduced, and true FOM --
against one another at a single shared macroscopic discretization, not to
pin down the panel's converged tip displacement in absolute terms, and
every row is nested at the same mesh precisely so that the comparison
among them stays meaningful regardless of where that mesh sits on its own
convergence curve.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Every item of the admissibility checklist in Section~\ref{sec:checklist}
is addressed with a closed-form argument rather than a numerical
penalty. (C1) holds for any energy-derivative stress definition; (C2)
holds because the model is built from $\bm C$ and $\Cof(\bm C)$; (C3)'s
actual content, tensor symmetry, holds by parameterizing only the three
independent stress components, while material symmetry ($D_4$) is
deliberately left unimposed; (C4) holds exactly, by an additive-constant
argument for the energy and by the balanced-direction cancellation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cancellation} for the stress; (C5), correctly
stated as Ball's \eqref{eq:poly-def} rather than the stronger, and for
this RVE false, SPD-tangent shorthand, is proved by the explicit convex
construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:polyconvex}, with the weaker,
correct rank-one necessary condition verified numerically; and (C6) is
supplied by the sixth-power directional terms together with the
logarithmic/quadratic volumetric barrier. None of (C1)--(C6) depends on
the loss weighting of Section~\ref{sec:loss}, an orthogonal, purely
statistical choice that happened to improve every reported test-trajectory metric, including $S_{12}$'s own componentwise error,
contrary to the naive expectation that dropping its dedicated weighting
should trade it away for a better fit of $S_{11}$, $S_{22}$. Any future
retraining that changes the relative magnitude of $S_{12}$ against
$S_{11}$, $S_{22}$, for a different RVE geometry or load protocol, say,
should revisit the component-stress weight rather than assume it
transfers unchanged.

The construction is also not tied to one convex architecture.
Section~\ref{sec:ickan} shows the identical proofs, and an equally clean
reference-stress cancellation at machine precision, carrying over to an
input-convex Kolmogorov--Arnold core, at comparable test-trajectory accuracy to the ICNN core with far fewer parameters.

The remaining two results sections make the case for a certificate,
rather than an accuracy check, from two independent directions, using
the four-tier ablation of Section~\ref{sec:c1-regression}: pure
regression, free hyperelastic ANN, polyconvex ICNN, polyconvex ICKAN.
Section~\ref{sec:c1-regression} shows a direct, quantitative violation:
the tier-1 pure-regression model has no energy potential at all, and a
trapezoidal cyclic-work test $\oint_{\mathcal L}\bm S\cdot d\bm\varepsilon$
around closed strain loops, refined to distinguish a genuine curl from
quadrature error, finds a stable, non-vanishing residual for it, and a
residual that vanishes under refinement for the three energy-based
tiers: a direct, measured failure of (C1). Section~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex}
shows the analogous failure one level up the hierarchy, for (C5): an
otherwise identically trained, uncertified flexible PANN (tier 2)
matches or slightly beats the certified models on every reported
accuracy metric, while failing the rank-one necessary condition of
polyconvexity on $\FreeRankOneViolationFraction$ of sampled states, at
ordinary, non-extreme deformations. In both cases the accuracy metrics
of Table~\ref{tab:master} are silent, or actively misleading: the tier-1
pure-regression model is in fact the most accurate of the four ANNs on
the test trajectory, by every reported metric. Accuracy and the
admissibility certificates simply answer different questions. Only a
direct, targeted test of each checklist item can confirm the certificate
is actually present.

The same distinction carries over to an actual structural solve.
Deployed as the macroscopic material law of a Cook's-membrane benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:cook}), the certified ICNN and ICKAN cores converge in
three Newton iterations at every one of twenty ramped load steps, up to
a genuinely large tip deflection; the free model, accurate on the same
test trajectory metrics as the certified cores, fails to converge at
the very first, smallest step -- a real structural consequence of the
missing certificate, even though a rank-one convexity check at the
exact Gauss-point states it visits finds no violation there, only in
the aggregate global sample of Section~\ref{sec:non-polyconvex}.
The certificate's practical value is not confined to the standalone
constitutive surrogate.

Nesting the projection-based ROM/HPROM surrogates of
Section~\ref{sec:rom-comparison}, and the true, non-reduced RVE FOM
itself, as the actual RVE-level solver at every macroscopic Gauss point
-- a genuine FE$^2$ computation, not a direct deployment -- confirms this
is not an artifact of deploying the certified PANN directly rather than
solving a genuine microscale problem: both ROM/HPROM surrogates converge
in the same three Newton iterations as the certified cores, once matched
to the arithmetic precision the small-strain regime requires, and the
same energy-free rank-one test, extended to all six model variants
without needing a potential, finds the same architectural split, zero
violations for the two polyconvex-by-construction cores and a real
double-digit rate for every model without that guarantee, sampled
broadly, even though none of the six shows a violation at the particular
states this specific structural problem visits. Comparing every model
directly against the true FE$^2$ answer this way, rather than only
against each other, further exposes a small-strain accuracy gap specific
to the certified cores, invisible to their own test-trajectory
metric and traced to how the training loss weights small-stress states;
one additional, cheap loss term, matching each model's own tangent
stiffness at zero strain to the RVE's true linear-elastic limit, closes
most of that gap without touching the admissibility construction itself.

\subsection{Algebraic redundancy of the \texorpdfstring{$Q^H$}{Q\^{}H} features}
\label{app:redundancy}
This expands Remark~\ref{rem:redundancy}. Writing $T=\tr \bm C$ and
$t_{ki}=\bm d_{ki}\cdot \bm C\bm d_{ki}$, the elementary $2\times2$
identity $\Cof(\bm C)=T\bm I-\bm C$ (the same identity behind
Lemma~\ref{lem:isotropic}, applied to $\bm C$ itself rather than to a
rank-one $\bm d\otimes\bm d$) gives $\bm d_{ki}\cdot\Cof(\bm C)\bm
d_{ki}=T-t_{ki}$, and hence, using $\sum_iw_{ki}=2$ and
$\sum_iw_{ki}t_{ki}=T$ (both consequences of the balance
condition~\eqref{eq:balanced-directions} established in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:isotropic-features}),
\begin{equation}
 Q_{k,2}^H=Q_{k,2}^F,\qquad
 Q_{k,3}^H=-T^3+3T\,Q_{k,2}^F-Q_{k,3}^F,
 \label{eq:redundancy}
\end{equation}
exactly, for every system $k$ and every $\bm C$ (verified symbolically and
confirmed numerically to machine precision for random balanced systems
and random $\bm C$). So the fifteen entries of $\bm z_{\rm pc}$
in~\eqref{eq:features} are not fifteen algebraically independent
scalars: given $I_1=T$ and $\{Q_{k,2}^F,Q_{k,3}^F\}_{k=1}^3$ (seven
numbers), the corresponding $\{Q_{k,2}^H,Q_{k,3}^H\}_{k=1}^3$ are fixed
exactly by~\eqref{eq:redundancy}.

The redundancy does not, however, make $Q_{k,3}^H$ useless as a
separate network input, for an architectural reason specific to the
monotone ICNN of Section~\ref{sec:c5-construction}: reconstructing
$Q_{k,3}^H$ from $T,Q_{k,2}^F,Q_{k,3}^F$ via~\eqref{eq:redundancy}
requires the \emph{negative} coefficients $-1$ on $T^3$ and $-1$ on
$Q_{k,3}^F$, which a network constrained to non-negative weights at
every layer (needed for the convexity-composition argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:polyconvex}) cannot represent internally.
Supplying $Q_{k,3}^H$ directly as its own coordinate therefore gives
the monotone network access to a combination of $\bm C$ that no
non-negative combination of the other coordinates could reconstruct,
even though it carries no additional information in the
unconstrained, purely algebraic sense: much as a monotone function of
$x$ and, separately, of $-x$ is strictly more expressive than a
monotone function of $x$ alone.

$Q_{k,2}^H$ is a different case, and we state this plainly rather than
let the argument above appear to cover it as well: the first identity
in~\eqref{eq:redundancy} has coefficient $+1$, with no negative sign
to obstruct reconstruction. A monotone network already has direct,
unconstrained access to $+Q_{k,2}^F$ with an arbitrary non-negative
coefficient, so assigning independent non-negative weights to
$Q_{k,2}^F$ and to $Q_{k,2}^H=Q_{k,2}^F$ is exactly equivalent to
assigning their sum to $Q_{k,2}^F$ alone. Supplying $Q_{k,2}^H$ as a
separate coordinate therefore changes nothing about the class of
functions the trained network can represent: unlike $Q_{k,3}^H$, it
is not merely redundant in information but redundant in
\emph{expressiveness} too. It is retained in the deployed architecture
for uniformity with the other per-system coordinates, not for any
representational benefit, and we report this plainly rather than
imply the same architectural justification covers both cases.

\subsection{A closer reading of Klein et al.'s trivial-group case}
\label{app:klein-trivial}
This expands the pointer in Section~\ref{sec:related-klein-linden}.
Klein et al.'s~\cite{Klein2026} own Table~1 lists the \emph{trivial}
group $C_1$ ($|\mathcal G|=1$, identity only) among the finite groups
their construction covers, and substituting $\mathcal G=C_1$ into their
group-symmetrization operator collapses the symmetrization sum to its
single, un-symmetrized term. Read this way, their own machinery already
contains the seed of a fully general, no-symmetry construction
satisfying (C1), (C2), (C4)--(C6), a nice illustration of just how
general their underlying integrity basis is. They do not state,
highlight, verify, or numerically exercise this reading anywhere in
their paper: every one of their worked examples (their Section~5) uses
the cubic group specifically. Crediting them with having solved the
general case would therefore over-credit an inference they never made
or checked, which is why the main text states our own contribution as
complementary rather than as closing a gap no other construction could
possibly reach.

\subsection{The periodic-boundary-condition averaging identity, in full}
\label{app:periodicity}
This expands the pointer in Section~\ref{sec:background-fom}. Under
periodic boundary conditions, a classical boundary-integral identity
(the divergence theorem applied to the periodic fluctuation field)
gives $\langle\bm F\rangle=\bar{\bm F}$ exactly, where $\bar{\bm F}$ is
the applied macroscopic deformation gradient and $\langle\cdot\rangle$
denotes the volume average over the RVE, and the same argument applied
to the microscopic first Piola--Kirchhoff stress gives a
volume-averaged macro-stress that is automatically energy-conjugate.
Because the Green--Lagrange strain $\bm E=\tfrac12(\bm F^T\bm F-\bm I)$
is a \emph{nonlinear}, quadratic function of $\bm F$, this identity
does not by itself give $\langle\bm E\rangle=\bm E(\bar{\bm F})$: the
two differ, in general, by a Jensen-type gap driven by the fluctuation
$\bm F-\bar{\bm F}$. The exact, elementary averaging identity that
transfers without approximation under periodicity is the one stated
above, at the level of $\bm F$, not of $\bm E$.

\subsection{A consistent tangent for the ROM/HPROM surrogates, and an energy-free rank-one test}
\label{app:consistent-tangent}
This expands the brief description in Section~\ref{sec:cook}. Neither
operating mode of Section~\ref{sec:rom-comparison}'s hyper-reduced pipeline
is used inside a Newton solve anywhere else in this paper
(Section~\ref{sec:results} only evaluates both along a fixed, already-known
test trajectory), so no tangent for either one is derived there.
Nesting them as the macroscopic material law of Section~\ref{sec:cook}
requires one: both the macroscopic Newton--Raphson iteration and the line
search it uses need $d\bm S_{\rm macro}/d\bm\varepsilon$, not only $\bm
S_{\rm macro}(\bm\varepsilon)$, at every Gauss point and every
macro-iteration.

Write the reconstruction map shared by both modes, from~\eqref{eq:decoder},
as $\bm u_f(\bm q;\bm\varepsilon)=\bm u_f^{\rm
aff}(\bm\varepsilon)+\mathbf V\bm q+\bar{\mathbf V}\mathcal N(\bm q)$, and
let
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf J(\bm q):=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm q}\big(\mathbf V\bm
 q+\bar{\mathbf V}\mathcal N(\bm q)\big)=\mathbf V+\bar{\mathbf
 V}\,\frac{\partial\mathcal N}{\partial\bm q}\in\mathbb R^{n_L\times n}
 \label{eq:appendix-J-manifold}
\end{equation}
be its own tangent, the curved-manifold analogue of the fixed $\mathbf
V_{\rm tot}$ used by the linear PROM; $\partial\mathcal N/\partial\bm q$ is
obtained once per query by automatic differentiation of the trained
decoder, the same tool already used for every constitutive tangent in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. D-HPROM--ANN sets
$\bm q=\bm\mu(\bm\varepsilon):=\mathbf A\bm\varepsilon+\bm c$, the fixed,
offline-fitted affine map of Section~\ref{sec:background-decoder} (the same
map used to initialize HPROM--ANN's own Newton correction); HPROM--ANN
instead solves the ECM-weighted, Galerkin-projected residual
of~\eqref{eq:ecm-residual}, restricted to the primary coordinate,
\begin{equation}
 \bm r_r(\bm q;\bm\varepsilon):=\sum_{g\in\mathcal Z}\mathbf J(\bm
 q)^T\bm B_{e,g}^T\bm S_g\big(\bm u_f(\bm q;\bm\varepsilon)\big)\,\omega_g\;=\;\bm 0,
 \label{eq:appendix-rr}
\end{equation}
for $\bm q^*(\bm\varepsilon)$ by Newton iteration, starting from
$\bm\mu(\bm\varepsilon)$.

\paragraph{D-HPROM--ANN: direct chain rule.} Since $\bm q=\bm\mu(\bm\varepsilon)$
is explicit, $d\bm u_f/d\bm\varepsilon=\partial\bm u_f^{\rm
aff}/\partial\bm\varepsilon+\mathbf J(\bm\mu(\bm\varepsilon))\mathbf A$
directly, with $\mathbf A:=d\bm\mu/d\bm\varepsilon$ the fixed offline
coefficient matrix. The macroscopic output itself is one further,
adaptively-weighted average, $\bm h(\bm\varepsilon)=|\Omega_0|^{-1}\sum_eA_e\,
\omega_{\sigma,e}(\bm\varepsilon)\,\overline{\bm v}_e(\bm\varepsilon)$
(either $\bm h=\bm S_{\rm macro}$ or the homogenized strain, both of this
same form, and both restricted in practice to $\mathcal Z_\sigma$), so a
second product rule applies,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d\bm h}{d\bm\varepsilon}=\frac1{|\Omega_0|}\sum_eA_e\Big[\frac{d\omega_{\sigma,e}}{d\bm\varepsilon}\,\overline{\bm v}_e+\omega_{\sigma,e}\,\frac{d\overline{\bm v}_e}{d\bm\varepsilon}\Big],
 \label{eq:appendix-outer-chain}
\end{equation}
where $d\overline{\bm v}_e/d\bm\varepsilon$ is element $e$'s own
Gauss-point mean of $\mathbb C_g\bm B_{e,g}(d\bm u_f/d\bm\varepsilon)_e$
($\mathbb C_g:=\partial\bm S_g/\partial\bm E_g$, the true microscale
Neo-Hookean tangent, already assembled at every Gauss point for the stress
itself), and $d\omega_{\sigma,e}/d\bm\varepsilon$ is the trained MAW--ECM
weight regressor's own Jacobian (Section~\ref{sec:background-ecm}), again
by automatic differentiation. Every factor above is closed-form or
autograd-exact; none is finite-differenced.

\paragraph{HPROM--ANN: the implicit step.} Now $\bm q^*(\bm\varepsilon)$
solves~\eqref{eq:appendix-rr} identically in $\bm\varepsilon$;
differentiating that identity and applying the implicit function theorem,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d\bm q^*}{d\bm\varepsilon}=-\Big(\frac{\partial\bm
 r_r}{\partial\bm q}\Big)^{-1}\frac{\partial\bm r_r}{\partial\bm\varepsilon}=:\mathbf
 K_r^{-1}\frac{\partial\bm r_r}{\partial\bm\varepsilon},\qquad \mathbf
 K_r:=-\frac{\partial\bm r_r}{\partial\bm q}\bigg|_{\bm q^*},
 \label{eq:appendix-ift}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf K_r$ is exactly the matrix the Newton corrector already
assembles for its own last update, $\Delta\bm q=\mathbf K_r^{-1}\bm r_r$: it
already includes the manifold's own curvature (a second-derivative
correction to~\eqref{eq:appendix-J-manifold}, since $\mathbf J(\bm q)$ is
not constant, absent from a Gauss--Newton approximation but present in a
full Newton step) and the residual weights' own dependence on $\bm q$, so
nothing further needs to be assembled for it. The right-hand side is
comparatively simple: the decoder and the residual weights $\omega_g$
depend on $\bm\varepsilon$ only through $\bm q$, so $\bm\varepsilon$ enters
$\bm r_r$ directly only through the affine lifting $\bm
u_f^{\rm aff}(\bm\varepsilon)$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\bm r_r}{\partial\bm\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\bm
 q}=-\sum_{g\in\mathcal Z}\omega_g\,\mathbf J(\bm q)^T\bm B_{e,g}^T\mathbb
 C_g\,\bm B_{e,g}\,\frac{\partial\bm u_f^{\rm aff}}{\partial\bm\varepsilon},
 \label{eq:appendix-drrde}
\end{equation}
the same already-assembled element tangent contracted with the affine
lifting's own, closed-form strain sensitivity ($\partial\bm
F/\partial\bm\varepsilon$). Once $d\bm q^*/d\bm\varepsilon$ is known
from~\eqref{eq:appendix-ift}, the outer chain
rule~\eqref{eq:appendix-outer-chain} applies unchanged, with $\mathbf
J(\bm q^*)\,d\bm q^*/d\bm\varepsilon$ in place of $\mathbf
J(\bm\mu(\bm\varepsilon))\mathbf A$: the two operating modes share their
entire outer, homogenization-level derivative, differing only in how $d\bm
q/d\bm\varepsilon$ is obtained.

Both tangents were checked against central finite differences on the real
trained models (not a synthetic sanity check) before use: relative error
$6\times10^{-4}$--$8\times10^{-4}$ for D-HPROM--ANN and
$4\times10^{-5}$--$2\times10^{-3}$ for HPROM--ANN, at strains at or below
this project's own training range; D-HPROM--ANN's degrades to a few percent
at strains far outside it ($|\bm\varepsilon|\sim1$, roughly twenty times
any strain Section~\ref{sec:cook}'s benchmark actually develops),
consistent with reduced trust in the trained decoder's own derivative that
far into extrapolation rather than an error
in~\eqref{eq:appendix-ift}--\eqref{eq:appendix-drrde} itself.

\paragraph{An energy-free rank-one test.} Section~\ref{sec:c5-implies}'s
rank-one check is stated as an energy second derivative,
$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}W(\bm F_0+t\,\bm a\otimes\bm b)|_{t=0}\geq0$, which
presupposes an energy potential $W$: unavailable for the tier-1 regression
model (Section~\ref{sec:c1-regression}) and for both ROM/HPROM surrogates,
which reconstruct stress directly. Write $\bm A:=\bm a\otimes\bm b$ and
$\bm F(t)=\bm F_0+t\bm A$; since
$\bm C(t)=\bm F(t)^T\bm F(t)=\bm C_0+t(\bm F_0^T\bm A+\bm A^T\bm
F_0)+t^2\bm A^T\bm A$ is exactly quadratic in $t$ (no higher terms), so is
$\bm E(t)=\tfrac12(\bm C(t)-\bm I)$, and its first two $t$-derivatives at
$t=0$ are closed-form and themselves $t$-independent,
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\bm E}:=\tfrac12\big(\bm F_0^T\bm A+\bm A^T\bm F_0\big),\qquad
 \ddot{\bm E}:=\bm A^T\bm A,
 \label{eq:appendix-Edot-Eddot}
\end{equation}
using $|\bm a|=1$ for the second. Define $\varphi(t):=\int_0^t\bm
S(\bm E(t'))\cdot\dot{\bm E}(t')\,dt'$, the work done along this
one-parameter path, well-defined whether or not $\bm S$ derives from a
global potential; when it does, $\varphi\equiv W(\bm F(t))-W(\bm F_0)$
exactly, so $\varphi''(0)\geq0$ is the same condition. Differentiating
$\varphi$ twice via the chain rule, using $d\bm S/dt=\mathbb
C\,\dot{\bm E}$ at $t=0$ and~\eqref{eq:appendix-Edot-Eddot},
\begin{equation}
 \varphi''(0)=\dot{\bm E}\cdot\mathbb C_0\cdot\dot{\bm E}+\bm S_0\cdot\ddot{\bm E},
 \label{eq:appendix-rankone-notangent}
\end{equation}
in Voigt notation throughout ($\dot{\bm E},\ddot{\bm E}$ with the usual
engineering doubled-shear convention, $\bm S_0$ undoubled, so every product
above is a plain dot product), needing only $\bm S_0$ and $\mathbb
C_0:=\partial\bm S/\partial\bm E|_0$ at the base state, exactly the two
quantities every law in this paper already returns. Validated against the
energy-based, double finite-difference method Section~\ref{sec:c5-implies}
itself uses, for the two certified cores that have both a potential and a
tangent to cross-check against,~\eqref{eq:appendix-rankone-notangent}
agrees to a relative error at machine precision ($10^{-15}$--$10^{-16}$),
before being applied, in Section~\ref{sec:cook}, to the three model
variants no energy-based check can reach at all.
